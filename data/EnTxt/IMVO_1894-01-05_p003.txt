On the Parliamentary TEMBULAND. contset now taking place in this
territory the Cape Mercury writesThere are now four politicians
soliciting the votes of the electors of the Transkeian constituency of
Tembuland. They are Mr. Molteno, Colonel Griffith, Advocate Simpson, and
Mr, T, A. King. We place Mr. Molteno’s name first because that is where
we hope to see it at the close of the poll. It was Sir Gordon Sprigg who
said that an in­dependent member was one on whom no dependence could be
placed. Certainly that is the condition of three who have been seeking
the suffrages of the electorate up to the present. Trying to sit on two
stools does not usually result in stability; and whatever preferences
might be for men of eastern rather western residence, we sink that
consideration in the larger uestion of party. A distinct attempt is to
be made to put Responsible Govern­ment on a proper footing, and either
that aim is good or bad. If it be good—as we think it is—then it is the
duty of every thoughtful and conscientious elector to cast his vote and
influence on the side of those who are pledcel to reform. The effort may
fail—Mr. Hofmeyr says it will —but its only chance of success is if Mr,
Innes and those associated with him obtain hearty and thorough support.
No wobblers wanted. Now Mr. Molteno is the only one ofthe quartet of
Tembuland wooers who is prepared to hoist his colours to the mast head
and sail honestly under them, therefore we express the hope that his
return will be ensured by those who think that politics is not a game of
skittles but a serious business. The last Parliament suffered from
flabbiness, and it is to be hoped that the next will be better in this
respect. Tembuland can assist materially in making it so.

It is with great grief that SAD SUICIDE, we record the death of Mr.

Nathaniel Daniel of Caba, Transkei, especially as he died by his own
hand. He had been for some days at the Tshingeni for the St. Mark’s
quarterly Teachers’ meeting. The dreadful news was brought to St. Mark’s
on Friday morning last, and the Archdeacon drove out with the Doctor and
the R. M-’s Clerk for the post mortem- What made the matter more sad was
to see how perfectly healthy every organ was—even the heart which was
thought, some time ago, to be diseased. During last year he had been
sus­pended for a time from Holy Communion for an outrageous exhibition
of anger at his own home, and when the news came his words were called
to mind, lie had said, in explanation, that he could not help it; that
at times, without any cause, he became frenzied; that he knew his head
was wrong, and he feared it was gettmg worse. Tiie doctor had pronounced
the suicide a case of temporary insanity, but as this was not judged
quite sufficient

the Archdeacon and the Rev. P. Masiza called a meeting of the relatives
and officers of the Tshingeni congregation on Saturday, and made an
exhaustive in­quiry into the circumstances preceding the death, and into
his previous history. It seems that in November a halter of his was
stolen from Mbulu. On December 19th he rode with his witnesses to the
Court at Tsomo, but the case was post­poned. Then a very good bridle was
taken at St. Mark's, and then a horse was miss­ing. On Tuesday last,
after settling up all his Mission business, he went to his mother's at
Tshingeni. There he began o be violent, saying that now his purse was
gone. They' sent for his brother William, who got him to saddle up and
start for home. In a little while he was back, and shouting that he
would have his money he attacked a lad, a cousin of his, so violently
that he is still laid up from the effects. His brother got him away, and
saw him once more on his way home. But he was back that even­ing, and
lay on the ground in the small hut he had been using. His mother
per­suaded him to lie on a mat, and got him some tea. Next morning he
went out early and never came back. He was found on Friday morning
hanging to a thorn tree in the bush. The Headman said that this violent
end had not surprised the people, and he gave an instance of a fit of
frenzy which had lasted an afternoon and all night till dawn. His
brother stated that when Nathaniel was at Tshingeni every whim jvas at
once complied with for fear of irritating him into one of these
outbreaks, which came on at times from the most trivial causes. After a
good deal more evidence of a similar nature all present agreed that,
though the poor man was sane enough during the intervals, he was not
responsible for his acts when the attacks of madness came upon him. On
Spnday tile Archdeacon came up to bury him, explaining to a large
congregation that though a suicide was a murderer, and no service could
be read over him, yet that in such a Vase as this the Church trusted
that God, in His mercy, had re­ceived the soul Of His afflicted servant,
and the funeral service was read. — The school at Caba came out best of
all those inspected by Canon Woodrooff'e last year, and Daniel was full
of hopes and plans for improving the school, enlaiging the building, and
helping in the work amongst the Amandungwana. We do not understand, but

ult. Mr. ROGERS said he be­longed to no party, while at Queenstown he

out of Parlia­ment as in it for ought of good he can achieve. We think
Mr. ROGERS gave up his trump card among the Native electors when,
because of the promise of Bond votes, he was ashamed to ally himself
with Messrs. SAUER, INNES and the new Party which will exert a by no
means inconsiderable power in the new Parliament; and we trust he may
still see his way back to the fold, if he is not going to make these who
vote for him look foolish.

[FROM A CORRESPONDENT.]

WHITTLE SEA, Saturday. — Dr. Berry had a very large meeting with Natives
at Kamastone on Thursday, December 28th. A number of Bond­men, working
'very hard among the Natives for their candidates, were also present.
They urge opposition to Dr. Beny on Scab and Dog Tax, and say that the
Doctor, not being a farmer, therefore is unable to know the Natives’
wants. One of them addressed the meeting against Dr. Berry. The latter
said he would wait the Commission’s report on Scab ; Dog Tax was a
matter for Divisional Council candidates. He fully dealt with local
boards of educa­tion and Native succession, was in favour of allowing
loyal Natives guns, and is a strong opponent of the Bond. The Natives
should not listen to what was whispered in their ears. He had not begun
taking an interest in Native questions yesterday, but for many years. He
demanded a restricted title for Native land—until parliament, and not
the government, should alter the same—at least for thirty-three years.

At Hackney there was a representative meeting. The Doctor maintained
that all questions of local interest were nothing to the great question
of Bond rule.

At Kamastone many Natives spoke favourably, and the meeting lasted four
hours. At Hackney it last three hours, and closed with three hearty
cheers for the Doctor.

NATIVE CONFERENCE.

QUEENSTOWN, Thursday.—Close on a hundred delegates were present at the
Native Electoral Conference on January 3rd. Mtombeni in the chair. Dr.
Berry was unanimously decided on; Mr. Rogers nominated by 49 votes, Mr
Frost 45, Du Plessis was thrown out at once. The meeting was afterwards
addressed by Dr. Berry who was enthusiastically received. It is
generally thought here that the result of this decision will be that Mr.
Du Plessis will be returned.
