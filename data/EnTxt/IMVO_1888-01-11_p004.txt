Reverend Charles Pamla in a Leader. Our con- temporary does not believe
in the sincerity of Mr. Pamla in avowing that he was a supporter of the
Sprigg Ministry. “With the wheedling unction (says the Cape Times) of

to be set down as in any wise opposed to Sir Gordon Sprigg’s ad-
ministration—as though Sir Gordon had anything to say in the policy
which goes by his name ; but this voluntary humility was of small

BELIEVING, as we do, that the Natives, who are the victims of this
persecution are qualified, what can be the object of Mr. Ingle and his
friends in trying to engineer them out of their rights by means of what
are at best mere legal quibbles. The Natives have never as- sisted in
returning a single member who was a discredit to the Victoria East con-
stituency, nor have they voted for a man who injured, in the slightest,
the interests of Mr. Ingle and his friends by blind, fanatic, advocacy
of the Natives’ cause. Mr. Rose-Innes whom they as- sisted in sending to
the present Parlia- ment, is a gentleman whose views and actions have
been alike fair towards Dutch, English, and Natives. Could anything
better be desired? What have our people done in the eyes of those who
are constantly harassing them in the exercise of their privileges as
citizens? The proceedings appear to us to be most unprincipled and
childish.

WE are very gratified to learn that at East London the efforts made by
the Native Vigilance Association to get the Natives registered have been
amply re- warded, and about a hundred Natives are enrolled. With this
result Mr. Lance, the Solicitor, retained by the Association has had
much to do. Both at Fort Jack- son and at Gwaba. where the Registering
Officers where at first slow to recognize the claims of the Natives, our
Solicitor succeeded before the Field Cornet’s Court to get Native
claimants put on the roll. It will be remembered that the Premier, on
introducing the Native Disfranchise- ment Bill in Parliament, made it
the subject of a special boast that in this con- stituency there was
only one Native on the list of voters. What we now see is the effect of
calling dry bones into life.

CONSIDERABLE interest has been evinced by the Herschel Natives in the
Registra- tion ; and quite a host of forms has been absorbed in this
district. A correspond- ent writes:—“ I never anticipated that there
would be such a demand for these forms at the last moment, and it would
have surprised you to see the way in which the men rolled up for them.”
The men, we are informed, mustered on the 5th January, to the tune of at
least 600, to substantiate their claims before the Registering Officer.
Mr. Halse represented the Natives. On the Registering Officer asking on
what grounds of qualification the claims were put in, Mr. Halse thus
ably stated the case 1st. That the district of Herschel forms part of
the Colony proper, and has been set aside as a Reserve for the Natives—a
Crown Reserve. 2nd. That the Natives holding ground and Huts, or
cottages in the Reserve by lawful autho- rity, that is the expressed
sanction of Government through their agents here, and who occupy those
lands and Huts, must be taken to be in actual occupation, as
contemplated by the 8th Section of the Constitution Ordinance. 3rd. That
Section 17, of Act 14, 1887, does not and cannot be made to operate in
the district of Hesrchel, since (See No. 2) there is no such tenure as
Tribal or com- munal Tenure obtaining in the district. 4th. That the 8th
Section of the Constitu- tion is not repealed, either wholly or in part,
inasmuch as it does not come into conflict with any of the provisions of
Act 14, 1887, and that the several claims are filed under the said 8th
Section. Mr. Halse contention seems to us to be very sound; and will, if
necessary be urged before a Superior Court. No decision, however, was
arrived at on Thursday last. Mr. S. E. Shawe, late of the Civil Service,
appeared to represent the Bond and to object to native claimants.

CORRESPONDENCE.

THE Native Vigilance Committee of Kamastone and Oxkraal have, we learn,
made a plucky fight over the names of our people who applied for
registration. Three gentlemen headed by Mr. Greeff, appeared on behalf
of the Bond to object to Natives, and inasmuch as no notice of the
objections had been filed, they were rejected. The Natives had put in
over a 100 claims. Of these, over CO were accepted, 'and over 70
rejected, on the ground that the latter did not produce receipt to show
that they had paid rates. The Native Committee thought that the
Registering Officer was asking them what was not necessary for the
purposes of the law, and resolved to contest the cases before the Civil
Commissioner. There are nearly 450 Natives registered in the Kamastone
and Oxkraal Wards of the Queenstown electoral division.

TUESDAY, the 31st instant, is the day fixed by our Civil Commissioner
for revising, amending, and settling the Registration Lists for this
division. Many Natives, who had sent in claims to Field Cornets,
especially in the wards over which Messrs. R. J. Crowe, Landrey, Moody,
and Stewart preside, will attend to hear their claims settled.

To the Editor of “ Native Opinion.”

SIR,—Allow me to correct what I think a little misleading in some
remarks in your issue of 4th inst., with regard to the Xalanga
Registration. As to the com- munal tenure it was admitted that
“occupation” was all that had to be considered if the tenure was not
com- munal. Titles of some lots were only referred to as evidence of
what the tenure was, not as proving occupation. I con- tended that even
the word “ communal ” introduced into these titles as it was did not
affect the tenure which was that of perpetual quitrent solely, the word
“ com- munal ” being descriptive of the lot only, not of the tenure.

This communal tenure question was what I did distinctly raise and I was
careful to have the Record of proceedings in such form that it would be
clearly raised by the Record itself before any Superior Court to which
the matter might be taken.

Then with regard to the quitrent; what I did was to drop it as the basis
of value when I found it could not be sustained as representing present
value. Mr. Levey fixed present actual value at 30s per morgen on the
equivalent of Is 6d per annum quitrent and I could obtain no evidence to
disprove this. Present value was undoubtedly what had to be taken and
was taken, and this being so no one was excluded on account of promises
to reduce quitrent in future. I only intro- duced any mention of
quitrent with a view to assisting the natives by means of a statement of
present value, to obtain a future reduction, and I do not think there
was any error dealing with the matter as I did. Yours truly,

W. F. LANCE.
