THE REP PANACEA.

MR BAILEY’S organ at Queenstown the Repre­sentative — seriously proposes
the removal of Natives from the roll as voters because, instead of
re­cording their votes for Mr. BAILEY, they deliberately gave their
sup­port to Messrs. PEACOCK and BRADFIELD at the recent election. It is
urged, too, as a ground of complaint that, “by the united “ effort of
the European section of “ the electorate, only one candidate “ out of
the three has been secured, “ the other two candidates having “ been
secured by Native suffrage.” Our contemporary finds it con­venient to
forget that this is the result of the cry, “ Plump for “ BAILEY.” We
remember dis­tinctly that the Representative and its friends were
reminded again and again by our esteemed collaborater the Cape Mercury
that there were three seats to provide representatives for. However,
they would not be warned. “ Plump “for BAILEY,” they apostrophised; and
“ Plump for BAILEY ” they did. The result is they have se­cured one seat
while the cleverer electors, who were solicitous for the due and proper
representation of the Circle, were considering how best to achieve their
object.

With the tremendous advantage that the “ plumping ” system gives to a
minority, completely baffling as it has done in the South Eastern
Province the plans of the majority, the wonder is that those who
“plumped for BAILEY” should not have placed their favourite far ahead of
Messrs. BRADFIELD and PEACOCK on the poll. That they have not succeeded
in doing so, shows that the minority that has “ plumped for BAILEY ”
must have been small indeed, and quite out of proportion to the noise it
made. As far as polling figures have come to light, they place the three
successful candidates so close to each other that it is difficult to say
what position each shall occupy when the total results arc forthcoming.
This fact alone is not favourable to the contention of the
Representative that Mr. BAILEY has been elected by the European section
of the electorate while Messrs. PEACOCK and BRADFIELD have had to depend
upon the Native vote qua Native vote. As a matter of fact the Natives,
as usual, find the English section of the community not agreed among
themselves as to whether Messrs. PEACOCK and BRADFIELD or Mr. BAILEY
should represent the Circle in Parliament. It must be borne in mind that
both the opposing parties arc Englishmen ; and as a disinterested party
the Natives come in to settle the dispute one way or the other. After
consider­ing the qualifications of the candidates they have
unhesitatingly given their award in favour of the party that says
Messrs. PEACOCK and BRADFIELD would well and ably represent the Border
in the L’pper House better even than Mr. BAILEY, grant him all the
qualifications he is said to possess. It now remains for the
representatives of the people

who assemble in the palatial buildings at the foot of Table Mountain to
judge, now that Mr. BAILEY will go down with Messrs. PEACOCK and
BRADFIELD whether the Natives had made the worse choice as their
detractors would have the world to believe. We could wish that after a
fair trial the House would take the earliest opportunity to make a
pronounce­ment in some regular manner on this point so as to settle the
matter as between those of the Representa­tive faith and the Natives.
But to talk of curtailing the voting power of a people simply because
they have done a plain statutory duty by recording their votes for whom
they please seems to us the very acme of absurdity. The Repre­sentative
and those whose views it represents are perhaps not aware that the
Natives are directly in­debted to the Sovereign in spite of the Cape
Parliament of the day for the franchise, such as it is. They owe no
thanks for the privi­lege to any Colonial Parliament. Indeed if it lay
in a majority of the Cape Parliament cither to con­tinue or to take away
the privilege from the Natives it Would have been withdrawn long ago.
The Representative Institutions which the Colony enjoy were granted on
condition that the Native shared the franchise as the only moans of
protecting his own interests ; and when it comes to be considered
Whether our people have abused the privilege or not, we are confident
Her Majesty’s Representative will acknowledge that no class of the
population has employed it to better advantage and in a manner
consorting with the dignity of the institutions of the Colony and of the
realm.

been running for the three seats in the Legislative Council, the Bond
has carried the day. Ono Independent can­didate could easily have been

HEALDTOWN INSTITUTION REUNION.

THE new Governor of this Institution, Rev. INSTITUTION R. F. Hornabrook,
is from all accounts the right man in the right place. Since he assumed
the manage­ment of affairs the Institution has mark­edly gained in tone.
The idea of in­augurating a Reunion of old students in connexion with
the Healdtown Teachers’ Association is a capital one. There are many
past students of the Institution, no longer teachers, who take a deep
interest in their Alma mater, and would gladly welcome anything to
establish a connecting link between them and the school. To such the
Reunion will be a perfect godsend. The directors of Institutions like
Healdtown are wise in fostering an espirit de corps among those who have
passed through their hands in connection with these Institutions. It is
mutually beneficial to the School and to the past students. We hope the
meetings announced to begin on the 31st instant will be a success.

THE VICTORIA EAST ELECTION.

MR. TAMFLIN opened the electoral campaign at Alice yesterday in
connection with the vacancy occasioned by the resig­nation of Dr, Pope
of the seat in the Assembly for the representation of this division. The
other gentlemen spoken of as candidates are Messrs. Thus. A. King of
East London, and Ballantyne a farmer residing near Alice. The Alice
Times, which has its fingers on the pulse of the electorate says that
while Mr. Ballantyne would receive “ fair support,” Mr. Tamplin is sure
to secure “consider­able support in the constituency.” So strong a
candidate is Mr. Tamplin, how­ever, that wo do not think anyone with any
experience of elections will venture to contest the seat against him;
much less Mr. Ballantyne, who retired in Mr. Tamplin’s favour at the
General Election. We understand Mr. Kingwill not enter the lists against
Mr. Tamplin. The chances are that this gentleman will have a walk over.
The date fixed for the nomination of candidates is the 21st proximo.

To say the least, IMVO has dealt very irrespectively with this

NATIVES AND THE CENSUS.

ADDRESSED TO THE EDITOR,

DEAR SIR,—In one of the paragraphs of your valuable paper, headed “
Notes of Current Events” of the 26 inst., you were thanking the
Government concern­ing the Census, and stated “ That then every
encouragement was afforded for the employment of Native teachers and
other educated Natives among their own people as enumerators.” Further “
you are sorry_ to hear in the Transkeian Magistracies that the wise
benevolent plans of the Government are being dis­torted by the local
Magistrates who are employing policemen and other Euro­peans to go among
the Natives.” I am No inform your readers that it is not in the
Transkeian Magistracies only where this wise scheme of the Government is
being distorted. For at a public meeting of headmen held at Lady Frere
on the 20th February, the Magistrate after having thoroughly explained
the purport of the Census, and the way it is to be done, in nominating
the enumerators, only two were Natives. And when the question was
put—Whether it is because there are no qualified educated Natives to be
appointed enumerators instead of Europeans? He replied “ That is no
question, for in case the enumerator do not know Kafir, an interpreter
will be appointed.” Most of the sensible men present were very much
discontented, for they well know that Natives are very suspicious of
European Agents moving among them. We fail to see why no reliable Native
young men have been appointed enumerators?

I have the honour to be, sir, yours etc.,

A NATIVE.
