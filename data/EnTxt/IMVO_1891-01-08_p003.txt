THE chances of the election of the Bond candidate for the Border
Province are far from good just now. The public are aware that Mr. David
N. de Wet of Aliwal North is the oflicial nominee ; but his rival Mr.
van Heerden of Tarkastad, urged by his friends and supporters at a
meeting recently held in that village has made up his mind to stand.
Thus a con­siderable number of Bond votes will be taken from Mr. de Wet
at the Queenstown electoral district. It is understood that yet another
section of that body in that neighbourhood will vote for Mr. T. Bailey
of Queenstown at whose instance he has taken the field. Then at
Wode­house, we are assured, many Bond votes will go to Mr. Bradfield who
is better- known there than the official candidate. It must be borne in
mind that the Bond is not strong in the Border Circle, and if the
adherents of that body in the divi­sions of Queenstown and Wodehouse are
voting in the manner stated Mr. de Wet, much less his rivals for the
Bond votes, have not the ghost of a chance of coming in.

ACCORDING to the Natal Mercury “ the elections for the Upper House of
the Cape Parliament in the Eastern Circles have evoked an unexpected
clement of re­sistance to the domination of the Afri­cander Bond.
Hitherto the Legislative Council has consisted very much of nominees of
that organisation, but on this occasion several “independent” candidates
have put themselves forward, with a fair prospect of success. Already
one Bond candidate has withdrawn in face of the strenuous opposition
organised against him, while another, Sir Henry Stockenstrom, seems
certain of being returned upon a platform of justice to native
interests—which throe years ago would have made his election an
im­possibility. Signs arc not wanting that the Bond has fulfilled its
mission, and will probably be replaced by some other form of patriotic
organisation—possibly that of the union of South Africa.” Our Natal
contemporary has shown itself in the past a fair judge of political
move­ments in this Colony; and there can be no doubt that in this matter
also it has given a correct view of the position as regards the Bond.

Kingwilliamstown Division Farmers’ Association, which bas appeared in

having these appointments abolished in the above named Division, From
his thorough knowledge of the Division, and his long and ripe experience
of Native matters, he found that the wonting of the system of Native
