The reply elicited by Mr. Chamberlain from the Imperial Government with respect to Matabeleland is highly gratifying. According to that reply Her Majesty’s  Government are prepared to send an , officer to advise Lobengulo. who would see that the interests of the Matabeles 1were not injured by concessions and e would advise the Chief to alter Mr. Rudd’s concession referring to arms and ammunition. As the future of the northward expansion movement, will depend much upon what the Imperial Government are doing now, it is to be hoped that they will be fortunate in hitting upon an officer who will be imbued with judgment and and discretion, who will facilitate the progress of that momentous movement. The Cape Mercury, the latest accession  to Ministerial journals, in attempting to  classify the three candidates for the Legislative Council vacancy according to  their political leanings, makes some J amusing blunders. “ All three,” re-  marks our contemporary, “are more or less pronounced supporters of the present Ministry.” Here the Cape Mercury is at issue with another Ministerial journal of ‘ longer standing. We mean the Free Frees, which has urged as a ground of its opposition to Mr. Hughes’ return that he belonged to the Opposition. Then again the public are informed by the Mercury that Mr. Gray has been discarded by those of the Sprigg household “ for his hot and avowed Ministerial partisanship which would appear to have blinded him to any possibilities of failure.” But the Watchman asserts the converse of the Mercury proposition for the shunting of Mr. Gray. For a Ministerialist he was too independent as witness his support of Mr. Warren at the General Election. “ Is it to be wondered at (asks the Watchman) if those who support the present Ministry and wish them to remain in office look upon Mr. Gray with suspicion, since, during the recent Assembly election, Mr. Gray, although professing to support the Ministry and in some instances proposing a vote of confidence in Colonel Schermbrucker, availed himself of the opportunity to canvass in the interests of Mr. Warren, a pronounced oppositionist. It should be borne in mind also that Mr. Warren has for some time been canvassing the constituency in the interests of Mr. Gray. Thus notwithstanding Mr. Gray’s statements at public meetings that he is a supporter of the Sprigg Ministry the above facts are sufficient to create a doubt in the minds of thinking men as to whether Mr. Gray is really a supporter of the Ministry or not. The Imvo says also that Mr. Gray is independent in his opinions, but we are at a loss to know what is meant by that statement. DoeS it mean that Mr. Gray is not to be depended upon as a firm supporter of the Ministry, as his action would seem to warrant electors in coming to such a conclusion.”

The spontaneous assembling of Natives in large numbers is not generally encouraged by Magistrates in the Native territories, as they almost invariably regard it as a sign of disaffection and war. Somehow or other the reported meetings of Natives to protest against the extension of the Circuit Court do not appear to have raised apprehensions in these officials. On the contrary they appear to be encouraging them. It is noteworthy that while this is the case private advices and our Native correspondence should go to show that the generality of the people regard the extension of the Circuit with favour and satisfaction.

A CONTRADICTION.

IT has been reported by some unknown authority that I am about to leave the good work in which I have been engaged for so many years. I beg emphatically to contradict the statement, and to say that when —from a breakdown in health, or some other cause—1 am likely to give up a work which has been so honoured and blessed of God in time past, I shall be sure to give due notice to the friends of our work, and of the girls. Till then the report may he considered untrue. I state this clearly, because it has caused some trouble and anxiety to the parents and friends of the girls under our care.
