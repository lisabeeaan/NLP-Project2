hopeful that if they had better arms they would be successful. Among our
people there is of course no wish to take arms against the white
population, and there is no probability of union among the Native
tribes. Not very long ago we met a European who had been a few months in
the country who with great warmth proceeded to denounce us as great
fools for attempting the combination which he assumed we were forming
against the European and for entertaining the hope that we can succeed.
We agreed to argue with him the following positions (1) Was there, as a
matter of fact, any attempt at such combination? (2) Was such
combi­nation probable. lie produced all the arguments which he said had
been given him by high authorities on Native sub­jects. After each and
all of these argu­ments had been demolished and reduced to nothing—their
real quantity—he fell back ou the position : “ But you cannot deny that
such a combination is possible.” We said no we cannot deny that any more
than we can deny that the Scotch can combine with the French against the
English, and we added that we are not aware that people live in dread of
.such possibilities and in so far as the cojnbi­nation of the Natives is
concerned we are confident that it can only be brought about by
Europeans, and they are in a position to make the combination
im­possible.

  This notice has already exceeded the space we intended to take and we
  close with commending the book once more to our countrymen especially
  and to our European friends who may wish to learn how to handle the
  Native Question if they wish their statements to have in­fluence with
  the Natives.
