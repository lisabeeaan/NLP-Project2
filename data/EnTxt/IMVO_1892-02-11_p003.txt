NATIVE OPINION

  T needed no prophet to foretell
  that the country would be in-

undated with the most reckless and
rabid writings founded on Mr. HOL-
LAND’S decision respecting the
Native claimants of Registration in
the Kingwilliamstown division. As
we have pointed out before, this
ruling is notorious for under-valuing
the landed property of the black
men by virtue of which these people
have been previously enregistered.
Their fellows in other Wards of
this district, however, are on the
register at the present moment, be-
cause they possess the qualification
which the Civil Commissioner of
Kingwilliamstown, by a method of
reasoning quite beyond our ken,
regards as no qualification at all.
A long array of Magistrates who
have graced the bench of the King-
williamstown Court have had, before
Mr. HOLLAND, to deal with the
values of the Natives’ erven with
the result that no doubt has ever
been cast on the value of the hold-
ing’s of the Natives under which
they have claimed to be on the Roll
as Registered Voters for years. For
a considerable period, too, the
local Divisional Council has claimed
taxes from our people on a valuation
of their plots ranging from £25 and
upwards. At the present moment
the Stutterheim Council valuation
of the land of the Natives of the ex-
tent of those which obtain in King-
williamstown district is £30, while
at Queenstown plots of a similar
character are put in as high as £60.
Mr. HOLLAND had abundant evid-
ence before him as to the ruling
annual rental of the Native’s arable

lands which comprise all that
was submitted for his judgement
and the annual rents were shown
to be at the average of E3
which at the usual twenty-years

purchase yields £60. Over, and
above, and behind all this, evidence
was led, and much more could have
been led before Mr. HOLLAND of
actual sales of the land in question,
transfer deeds being registered in
the Civil Commissioner’s own office,
and these transactions show that fair
purchasers have given from £25 to
£35 for their ground. In the face of
such overwhelming and, as we hold,
conclusive evidence, Mr. HOLLAND
elected to follow Mr. BROSTER’S
testimony, which, if anything, was
of an extremely hypothetical des-
cription. This gentleman’s evidence
before Mr. HOLLAND was to the
effect that £6 would be a fair value
for these six acre lots. But before
Mr. W. B. CHALMERS, this same Mr.
BROSTER appraised this very land at
£16 ! Yet Mr. HOLLAND preferred
his evidence in accepting the small-
er amount; and then did what, in
the face of facts relating to actual
sales and current rents, we cannot
help characterising as an injustice
to our people who stood openmouthed
with astonishment at hearing that
their lands for which they could get
at an average of £30 were con-
sidered at a court of justice dear at
£6!

Encouraged by this result the
Kaffrarian Watchman assisted by a
chorus of annonymous, nondescript
scribes, is vehemently declaiming
against the Field-Cornets who, to
the best of their lights, assessed the
Natives holdings at what they
deemed the correct worth. It is
even suggested that the Superior
Courts should be moved to strike
off the people placed on the Register
by these conscientious fairminded
men. In the interests of justice
and right there is nothing Natives
would welcome with greater delight
than the trial of these vexed ques-
tions before a Higher Court; and
our contemporary might assist our
people, who we believe have been
greviously wronged, in getting this

subject of what is the real and fair
value of property before the East-
ern Districts or the Supreme Court.

We observe, further, that Ons
Land, the new Dutch newspaper,
has thought fit to base conclusions
on the Kingwilliamstown Registra-
tion Court. This is perfectly
natural. But our contemporary,
which strives to be fair and moder-
ate, is bound to reconsider and
withdraw its harsh and sweeping
language applied to respected Mis-
sionaries like Rev. E. J. BARRETT
of Annshaw and those whom it calls
“negrophilists,” when it re-examines
the matter by the impartial
light we have endeavoured to throw
on these proceedings ; will acknow-
ledge the purity of their motives;
and agree also that, under the cir-
cumstances there was no disposition
on the part of the Natives and their
friends to place fictitious values on
these peoples lands; more- es-
pecially as they did but follow
previous decisions of Mr. HOLLAND’S
predecessors.

  HILST everybody awaits
  with no small anxiety the

  Ministerial pronouncement on the
  Franchise question, its discussion
  loses nothing of the zest with which
  it has been invested during the past
  few months. But the most singu-
  lar thing about the controversy is
  the fact that the more the issues,
  as raised by Mr. HOFMEYR, are ex-
  amined the more one fails to com-
  prehend what it is that the Member
  for Stellenbosch wants exactly ; and
  it is only by speculating on what
  may be his ulterior aims that it is
  at all possible to understand him.

  our pen has written often, and, as
  we trust and hope, to the point.
  For by taking the Bond Leader at
  his word, our misfortune has been
  that of those who argue in a circle;
  in tying to follow Mr. HOFMEYR’S
  arguments it has always seemed to
  us that we ended were we began.

  Take, for instance, his plural vote
  scheme, proposed with the avowed
  intention of getting the so-called
  “ barbarians ” under. Mr. HOF-
  MEYR proposed to give two votes to
  those holding property to the
  amount of £75, while he is unwill-
  ing to take away the vote from
  those who qualify for the £25
  franchise. In the retention of the
  £25 we see Mr. HOFMEYR’S states-
  manship, as he knows full well that
  Her Majesty’s Government would
  not sanction the disfranchisement
  of the very lowest of the Queen’s
  subjects for a whim—not even at
  the instance of the almighty Bond.
  But the attitude of Mr. HOFMEYR in
  this respect is at once the strong
  point and the weak point of his
  statesmanship. According to his
  own showing the £25 franchise
  allows “ the barbarians,” who out-
  number the whites in the propor-
  tion of from one to four, to swarm
  the Register. Nevertheless he does
  not propose that they should be
  swept away ; but instead calmly pro-
  poses the simple doubling of the
  votes of the white minority, a course
  which—granting “ the barbarian ”
  bogey to be a real, living, fact; and
  not a dead, lifeless, thing that it
  really is—will not avail one whit.
  For, as twice one makes two,
  and two is still below four, “the
  “ barbarians ” will still remain
  masters of the situation, unless the
  votes of the minority are quin-
  tupled ; and even then, in as much
  as there will be propertied “ bar-
  barians” qualified for the £75,
  the ascendancy of “ barbarians ”
  remains unshaken.

  But, coming down from Mr. HOF-
  MEYR’S utopia to the every-day
  matter-of-fact things, we know that
  “ the barbarian ” on the Register,
  on whom the Member for Stellen-
  bosch so lustily rings the changes,
  is a myth ; and he is dangled before
  the simple among the European
  electors, while a number of seats
  are being annexed by, and for pur-
  poses of, the “ National Party,” of
  which he is himself—well, not the
  master. In another article we hope
  to demonstrate the equal futility of
  the simple raising of the Franchise,
  which finds favour with those who
  ostentatiously avow that they are
  the “ moderate party.”
