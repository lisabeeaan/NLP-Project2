Topics of the Day.

WE regard it as a sign of WIDER VIEWS, the widening of the views of the
average Colonial elector when a Farmers’ Association, such as that of
Molteno, resolves unani­mously to ask a leading politician like Mr.
Sauer to speak at Molteno on his way to or from his Aliwal North
constituents. The complaint hitherto has been that there were not as
many opportunities for a public man to speak in this country as in
England. The Cradock farmers inviting Mr. Merriman, and the Molteno
farmers’ request to Mr. Sauer show that this is fast passing away,

THE article we take over AS OTHERS from the Natal Witness SEE us, on the
Natives Vote is of special interest at the pre­sent time. There is no
question that if our people had a great say in the compo­sition of the
Cape Parliament it would soon be dominated by the most capable men to be
found in the land. The refer­ence of our contemporary to Mr. Scberm-
brucker should be read as implying that but for the Native votes cast in
for “ the Colonel ” that genial and volatile poli­tician would not
to-day be the representa­tive of the intelligence of King Williams town.

THE Alice newspaper in- GARBLED. forms its readers that “IMVO considers
Victoria East should be well satisfied with its present members of
Parliament.” This, we venture to describe as a half-truth, since it is
not only this journal but all the newspapers of the country have
expressed themselves in favor of the course indi­cated.

HERE is another garbled YET statement of the Alice ANOTHER Times: —“
There is a HALF-TRUTH, strong feeling in this dis­trict to return a
local man man to Parliament.” It would have been more correct to have
added that the “strong feeling” is surging in the bosoms of one or two
aspirants for Par­liament and one or two agents who wish to see things
hum a little. For the rest the bulk of the voters, more especially in
Peddie, are quite content with the able men they have as their
representatives.

THE TEMBULAND CONTEST.

OUR countrymen in Tembuland
have decided upon holding

a conference, probably at Cala, on
the 20th of next month to decide
upon the candidate whom they
should support in the forthcoming
election. Messrs. GRIFFITH, KING,
SAMPSON, and any other can-
didates for Parliamentary honors
are expected to attend and
address the meeting. Afterwards
the conference will indicate the
candidate of its choice. That the
people should meet and endeavor to
agree upon common action, we can
understood well enough; and can
even commend the step. But the call-
ing together of rival candidates, to
display their eloquence or lack of
it seems to us a harking back to the
ancient times, when the Greek
civilization was extant, and printing
was an unknown art. And certain-
ly there is nothing to be gained,
while a great deal might be lost,
by thus bringing together opposing
candidates. The opportunity would
be useful to the most glib tongued
to outbid men, perhaps far and
away his betters at heart, who,
nevertheless, may have neglected
forensic oratory in their studies.
The past careers of Messrs. GRIF-
FITH, KING and SAMPSON are now
pretty well known ; and if there
be any uncertainty as to their
political actions in the future our
people have the certain test of
finding out by the company which
they shall keep in public affairs
whether they are sincere or other-
wise. For our people may depend
upon it that a candidate may say
nicely a number of things on which
the people feel strongly; but the
test is in the mode of carrying his
purposes out. Thus, as an illustra-
tion, no faith need be placed on the
candidate who promises an excise,
a satisfactory Native land settle-
ment, and the prohibition of the
sale of liquor to Natives, but says
he hopes to carry those things out
by allying himself with Mr. HOF-
MEYR, Sir GORDON SPRIGG, Mr.
RHODES, and other men who do not
believe in these things; implying
at the same that he would be
against Messrs. SAUER, INNES and
others who are known for years to
have held decided views on these
matters. The three candidates
before the electors of Tembuland
are known by everyone to be un-
satisfactory on this head; and no
amount of speaking and white-
washing can hide the fact. The
best course then for those who do
not hold their political views cheap-
ly is to cast about for a candidate
I who, in addition to enunciating fine

views, will be honest and straight­forward enough to boldly avow himself
as of those who hold like views with himself in the House; for the
trimmer and the jellyfish cannot be depended upon for two months
together. He may be found wherever it suits his personal interests to
send him, and on such political prostitution every voter who means
business will cry— Avaunt!

The question of contesting the Port Elizabeth seats in the forthcoming
election, says the Eastern Province Herald, has not altogether fallen to
the ground. A meeting was held the other night to consider the question,
when it was decided to approach the following gentlemen: —Messrs. R.
Davidson, J. Searle, and A. Dickson, with a view to see if they would
receive a deputa­tion on the subject.

A Queenstown correspondent of Ons Land wires that the Bond in that
district has nominated as its candidates Messrs. M. J. du Plessis and
Rogers. This is a dodge to spoil the game of the forward farmers and
townspeople to return Mr. Frost with Dr, Berry; the evident object being
to ally the supporters of Mr. du Plessis with those of Mr. Rogers. Mr.
Rogers is too old a bird not to see the snare of the fowler. If
unfortunately, he should not see it, then those who would otherwise
support him are wise enough to see through the dodge and will save him
if he cannot save himself.

At last Uitenhage has found a gentleman to run in double harness with
Mr. Mackay in Mr. H. W. Bidwell. Well that gentleman has been too long
in making up his mind; and it should not be forgotten that it was Mr.
Bidwell who stood in the way of the more “forward” electors when Mr.
Rautenbach was returned without op­position at the election, —by
deserting his guns at the last moment. We trust the reasons which made
him show the white feather then have disappeared now, and that he may
come romping in with Mr. Mac­kay. Considering that last time Mr. Tudhope
and Mr. Mackay were returned by four figures, while the Bondmen scored
500 and 600, these gentlemen should have a good chance as things have
not changed much from what they were then.

Mr. George A. Hay has taken the field with very good prospects in King
Williams Town. His friends have hurriedly got up a requisition to him to
start his candidature; and he has prepared one of the tersest and best
replies out. There is no question he is carrying a large number of
people who hold that the representation of King Williams Town is sadly
in want of ballast by one member at least.

NATIVES AND COURTESY.

The spiteful and bitter little attack of the Watchman respecting Mr.
Jabavu’s good humored remark about the illeg­ibility of Mr. Holland’s
own signature— when the Magistrate was ruling Natives off the Electoral
Roll for this cause— has done the editor of the Imvo good rather than
otherwise. .The Kimberley Advertiser followed the cheap popularity plan
of writing about “ Native Impu­dence ” whereupon Mr. John Storey writes
a manly letter, in the course of which he says:—

“ I am very glad, Mr. Editor, that there is one Native in the country,
at least of Mr. Jabavu’s education and practical common sense, and that
he has the courage of his convictions and dares to tell them out, I
trust he will long be spared to use his voice and pen in defending the
interests of his fellow- countrymen, and to fight lor their right­ful
privileges, and that he will be a powerful influence in helping to build
them up into an industrious and enligh­tened people.

In conclusion, Mr. Editor, I should like to remind you that it was this
same Mr. Tengu-Jabavu that you so highly commended and praised so
deservedly when he recommended the Native voters of Kimberley to give
their sup­port to Mr. Solomon rather than to Mr. Cowie, which showed his
sound com­mon sense, the wisdom of that choice being more than justified
by that gentle­man’s clear, honorable, and statesman­like conduct during
the last session of Parliament.”

There is little to add to this. Mr. Jabavu’s crime is the very serious
one of holding to certain political principles. As for habitual courtesy
we wish that the exponents of an opposite political creed had a trifle
more of it; and when they wish to chronicle things set an example to the
Native by generously erring on the side of fairness to their opponents.
Mr. Jabavu has two courses before him to secure the regard of his
detractors. Either to turn his skin white or his principles black! —
Cape Mercury.

CAPE NATIVE VOTE-

{Natal Witness.)

In the electoral division of Queenstown there are several Farmers’
Associations, and these have been holding meetings for the purpose of
appointing delegates to attend a conference at which there will be a
nomination of candidates for representation of the district in the House
of Assembly. At all these asso­ciations, with one exception, a proposal
was carried that the Native voters should be directly represented at the
Conference by one of themselves, Mr. Tengo-Jabavu, the able and
well-educated editor of NATIVE OPINION, being selected. In the
Queenstown division there are 800 Native votes which have been used with
great judgment and generally in support of the best candidates. Mr.
Innes, the ex- Attorney-General, owed his first seat in Parliament to
the large Native vote in the division of Alice. Colonel Scherm­brucker
had the solid Native vote at the last election for King Williams Town,
and we are not aware of a single instance in which its exercise has been
an abuse. Nor have the Native electors ever lent an ear to demagogues of
their own race, of whom there have been a few, nor shown any wish to be
represented by other than Europeans. The Tylden Association only refused
to have a Native at the conference by a majority of one, whilst all the

reasonable. It is worthy of notice that no request had been made by the
Natives, and that it was an entirely spontaneous act of the farmers, who
