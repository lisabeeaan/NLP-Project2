Soon after the Emperor sent a letter
to the King of Prussia, saying, “As I
cannot die at the head of my army I
lay my sword at the feet of your
majesty.”

On the receipt of the news in Paris
a republic was declared and political
amnesty proclaimed.

Metz, Strasburg, Phalsburg, Toul, and
Montmedy still hold out. The Prussians
however are close upon Paris, and com-
munication with that city was likely to
be cut off.

On the 11th September the Emperor
was kindly received by the King of
Prussia, and the French officers were
released on their parole, and the men
marched into Germany.

The Empress Eugenie behaved nobly
in adversity. She said “ she preferred pity
to power, and would not oppose the
declaration of the Republic.” She has
left France and passed through Belgium
on her way to England with her son.

The Italians are preparing to enter
Rome, and their troops are near that city.

Russia is reviving the Eastern question
with Turkey, arranging her arsenals,
and preparing for the transport of troops.

The Captain turret ship has gone down
in a gale off Cape Finisterre with nearly
all hands numbering 500 men. Captain
Coles her inventor was on board.
