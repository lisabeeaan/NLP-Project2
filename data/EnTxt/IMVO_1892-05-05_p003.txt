Native Opinion,

THE Bond Congress is no longer the “ big gooseberry ’’ of the year;
indeed,, judging from the space given to it in the metropolitan papers,
it may be classed with the “ very small potatoes.” One mem­ber did
indeed claim for the Stellen­bosch gathering the full authority of
parliament, but the remark was pas­sed by those who do assemble at the

Cape St. Stephens, no doubt with the feeling of “Ah, my dear boy, you’re
not in it.” Of course there was a discussion on locations, squatters,
vagrants, and the everlasting pass law, but the Cape Argus boils the
potato down into a small compass Mr. VAN NIEKERK, a delegate, as it
appears, from Herbert, wanted to see “ that issuers of passes and owners
of landed property should issue passes to which there should be a
counterfoil so that they could be traced but his resolution to that
effect was lost, and well it might be seeing that in the course of a
long discussion “ bitter complaints were made that the Kafirs forged
passes for stolen horses and cattle, and that dishonest white men in the
Herbert” —note that, in the Herbert—“ and other districts supplied
Kafirs with passes so that they could convey stolen cattle from one
district to another, which were bought by the former (that is by the
dishonest white men) far below their actual value.” If counterfoil
passes were kept of course these white men might be brought to justice,
and yet the resolution to have this convicting record was lost. We
wonder if our people taught these white men to steal, or if Natives
learnt it from their masters. On this the Con­gress was probably silent.
But we have it now, on the high authority of the Bond, that white men m
more than one district are cattle thieves, and that they incite our
people to steal, even adding to their guilt another crime, that of
forging passes. That our people should steal when taught by white men we
regret, but cannot be surprised at. White people are much given to set
themselves up as models ; and it is even said of some of our people ‘‘
He is nearly as good as a white man ” Such phrases now appear to require
careful consideration. What we regret in the interests of our people,
who are being Jed astray, is that no resolution was carried—or even
proposed—to the effect “ that every member of the Bond be required to
assist in bring­ing these dishonest white men to justice-.” Surely the
leaders of the Bond are not going to ensure the Governor and Ministers
(Mr. SIVEWRIGHT specially, we presume,) for travelling on Sundays, and
then leave white thieves who demoralize the Natives, to pursue their
nefari­ous business undisturbed.

THE FRANCHISE.

THAT the Government Fran- chise proposals will go through the crucible
in Parliament is be­coming clearer and clearer as the time of the
assembling of that body draws near. One of the fires be­tween which the
suggestions ex­plained by Mr. SAUER at Aliwal North must pass is that
kindled by the Farmers at Cradock, on the one hand, in the form of a
resolu­tion directed against any alteration ; and the other consists of
that of the Bond, which is still rivetted to the plural vote. If both
these sections are sincere—a point which we are not at present in a
position to dis­cuss—there can bo no doubt that the debate on the
Franchise next session will not to be the tame affair which many people
anticipate it will be The question will resolve itself into one of
general ship, and the leader endowed with sufficient strategy in the
Parliamentary fight will achieve success. In regard to the forces that
the exigencies of next session will call into existence, a question has
been addressed to us by the Independent (Kimberley) as to who will be
the leader of the revolt against the Franchise reform. We should say
that the man most in earnest on the matter must naturally take the
leadership. That man is not far to seek. The Gallant Member for East
London, Captain BRABANT, has shown his sincerity, if in nothing else, at
all events, in this matter of the Franchise. He is a capable and
effective debater whose singleness of purpose and in­dependence are
recognised through­out the land, and we should very much like to see his
resources as a commander of men transferred from the bloody field of
battle to the equally important, though bloodless, struggles associated
with Parlia­mentary campaigning. Captain BRABANT’S name comes readily to
our mind by reason of the speeches he has delivered lately, more
espe­cially the one made at the East Lon­don Oddfellows’ Dinner last
week, in which he strongly urged the organisa­tion of a political force
to counteract the Bond on the Franchise business. It would bo the wisest
and best thing to do to give him a free hand while under the
inspiration, and were the Eastern members to trust him as commander the
frontier districts would get more out of this franchise controversy than
Mr. HOFMEYR bargained for.

THE sittings ofthe Wes- WESLEAN leyan Conference, which CONFERENCE was
held at Cradock this year, were brought to a close on Wednesday week. In
the judg­ment of those who are frequent attend­ants of those gatherings
it is ranked as the most successful Conference that has been held. The
work it transacted Was considerable and it had to deaf with some
questions of no small moment. All were dealt with in the most perfect
temper, and, as far as present lights serve the best decisions wore
arrived at. The veteran missionary, Rev. Jas. Scott, who had the honour
to preside on so unique an assembly is to be congratulated on the way in
which he conducted the affairs of the Conference; and it is to bo hoped
this auspicious commencement of his term of office as President of the
Conference may characterise the whole year. Indications are not wanting
that it will be so, as wit- ness the signal services Rev. Thomas Cook
has begun at Port Elizabeth. Rev. James Thompson, M.A., has been
desig­nated President for next year.

THE following is the A NATIVE ON substance of a speech by LABOUR. a
Native at an election meeting in Victoria East recently These candidates
seeking elec­tion for Parliament are so accommodating while they are
canvassing. They promise all manner of good things that they would do
for us, but when they get to Parlia­ment they forget all. When we go to
work on the railway these very white people kick and maul us about, and
when you go and coinplain to the Magistrate, full of bruises, you are
told to bring an agent, and if you say you have no money you are simply
told nothing can be done for you. Yet they say wo refuse to work. Even
nearer home, if you happen to point your finger at a white man you are
taken to the Magistrate, and he, without verify­ing the correctness of
this statement, gives you eight months imprisonment because the white
man’s word is believed before a black man’s. We are tired of these
elections every year, and our state never improving, notwithstanding the
many promises.

  ACCORDING to all accounts THE BOND the Bond Congress held

CONGRESS. this year at Stellenbosch

was not only barren of re­sults, but positively fruitful of dissensions.
To such an extent was this the case that even the Cape Argus, which has
for some time past taken it upon itself to pat the association on the
back is not slow to say that the Bond had outlived its days. On many
questions its decisions were halting and hesitating. The Franchise does
not seem to have evoked the interest it has done before, possibly
because the Minis­terial plan which seems to have satisfied the whole
country, does not promise the immediate results that were expected from
the Bond proposals of last year which are the same as those fathered by
Mr. Hofmeyr in the House. Nevertheless a feeble show of consistency was
made in the affirmation of the resolution of the Kimberley Congress. We
shall see what efforts will be put forward to carry it out in Parliament
over the heads of Ministers who are against the dual vote. Perhaps the
most significant act of the Congress is the virtual removal of Mr.
Hofmeyr from the leadership of the party and vesting his duties in a
committee of three mem­bers of Parliament consisting of the President of
the Congress for the time being and two others to be nominated by him.
Mr. Hofmeyr, for reasons patent to every one who considers the matter,
has been opposed to this. It has, notwith­standing, been carried. It
remains to be seen how he will regard so gratuitous an insult to his
successful leadership of the party for the past ten years.
