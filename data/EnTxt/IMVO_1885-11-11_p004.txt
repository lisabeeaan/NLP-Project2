Prejudice, what is it ? the word of itself means plainly no more than a “judgement formed beforehand ” but as it is used now in regard to each other it means “ an unfavourable anticipation about one,’’ so that prejudice against natives as a race is that ‘unfavourable anticipation’ about them regardless of the evidence that should point out this and that man or woman as unworthy of regard. Wherefore this prejudice? Because the majority of the natives me said to be dishonest and look upon the plunder- ing of farmers as a species of gallantry and no crime. I grant that this was true of natives of days gone by, but now, he who filches from even a Dutchman is looked upon as a thief by hie fellows; this is what they say of this at the kraals, “ then, my young men, we were enemies with the whiteman, now, we are all subjects of one man, the Government.” “If you take from the property of another, you are amenable to the law and disgrace not only yourself but the head of your Clan. This is the admonition of the old men to the young, and though it might be urged against it, that it is systematically disregarded, allow me to retort, what nation in the world is crimeless? Why is there a necessity for the institutions of judges and prisons even in venerable old England? To cut short any necessary remake on this question let me humbly submit, my dear reader that, the prejudice against races of which I have been treating in these Rambles is a natural consequence of one race being subject to another; and that if the native man or woman presumes to come with all the accretions of barbarism unprooned, and expects acceptance in a highly civilised society he is mistaken. He or she must relinquish, not only all authority and power, as that is frequently done without consulting him for the convenience of the company, but also all that is obnoxious to and detracts from the happiness of civil society. Dis- appointment he must expect and shall encounter, but he must guard against this by being loyal to all fellow subjects and dutiful to the powers that be. This, I submit, would be a penacea for all our mutual misgivings, and diminish the matters in difference to all concerned. What! will the rather submit to the son before the son make obeisance unto him ? No, the son must submit first and the father will be propitious unto him afterwards, that is plainly and simply situation, for what nation in the world is there so callous that it will not reward merit ? None at all, nor will a British Colony be guilty where all the world innocent. Pardon me for this long diversion, my dear reader, and I will go on. Next morning we started from Queen’s Town by train bound for Burghersdorp. The line was not opened to Aliwal North then. But it will be opened in the course of these Rambles and you shall be glad to see it as I saw it. I won’t tell you how it looked just now, or you will be getting me spoil my story by a relation of the event. From Queen’s Town to what I suppose they call a ' Poort ’ the line is as straight as a walking stick. Oh ! I am forgetting about my “First Class Ticket,” on examining my ticket the officer cast a bland smile at me, with a leer said ‘ it’s alright.’ I did not ask him what alright? but as he handed to me the ticket I took it, and went to my third class carriage with my luggage. As 1 walked up and down the line of the train, pretending to be at home, the same officer, after a short con- fab with the Station Master, came up to me and announced to me in blandest manner possible, evidently restraining a hearty fit of laughter within his jaws, that he had a first class carriage which I have all to myself alone, putting a spiteful emphases on the word alone, I declined the kind offer and said, thank you I prefer where there is company. After leaving the station, and on looking back on Queen’s Town and its environs and native location, I did not wonder at the most stringent regulations regarding natives living in the location which wore in vogue some time since and which were executed with the greatest severity. The location is simply immense out of all proportion to the town that feeds it, countaining, as it must be presumed all characters that make life a burden. From the Poort on to Lessyton and Sterkstroom the engineer has constructed the line with a sorcerers skill. On looking at Lessyton and the pleasant looking Basin in which it is situated, the buildings huddled together in defiance of all straight lines and right angles, I wondered that missionaries had not formed building societies as an accompaniment to the godliness which they preach! What a pleasant discovery it would be to all. Lessyton in South Africa, the scene of the Deserted Village of Goldsmith!!
