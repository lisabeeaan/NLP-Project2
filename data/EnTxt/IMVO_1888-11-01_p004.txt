Messrs. WARREN and SOLOMON on the other hand, at the beginning received
with respect, at the end were enthusiastically cheered. They show­ed
clearly that they were true friends of the frontier, and that they had a
definite and sound policy—not to tie themselves up to any Ministry but
to watch over our interests in Par­liament, and obtain in an open and
honest way what is required for the development of this part of the
country, having due regard for the Colony as a whole. Both members spoke
well. Mr. SOLOMON’S speech was one of the most statesmanlike addresses
ever delivered in this town. It showed clearly that he only wanted a
little time to prove that he was a coming man ; ’ and all parties ad­mit
that had the show of hands been taken when he sat down a very different
result would have been manifest.

THIS division is under great obligation to Mr. Merriman for his letter
in yester­day’s Mercury. He shows very clearly that the Colonel’s “
facts ” are only as true as a novel. That the wholesale reduction in
expenditure is only another way of saying “ more waste.”

IT is well that the Prime Minister went home, because, while he was
here, it was confusing to hear first Sir Gordon and then the Colonel
proclaim himself the only genuine friend of the frontier, and the only
one in Parliament who had any ability. Now the Commissioner has it all
to himself, and according to our contem­porary, the Mercury, this is the
result.

A PRETTY little incident occurred at the Nomination on Tuesday which was
quite lost upon the crowd. At the conclusion, Mr. Dick, senr., leaned
over and said in a loud tone to the Commissioner, “ Can yon not give
some assurance that the Government will tax brandy.” To which Mr.
Schermbrucker, with a shrug, replied

My dear fellow, I am only here as a candidate, and I cannot pledge the
Go­vernment.” Mr. Dick did not see that any such statement from the
Commis­sioner would lead to a polite little note from the Premier
requesting formal re­signation. The Ministry is awaiting instructions.

THE Colonel went to the nomination at head of a strong following,
marching two and two—just as if they were going to a funeral. The
Colonel left the Court House in company with Mr. Solomon. Even Dr. Egan
was left to take care of himself. Coming home from a funeral was
sug­gested. It is suggestive, indeed pro­phetic.
