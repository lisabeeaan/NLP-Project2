THE NEW PARTY,

REFERRING to the new combination of which there were but indications
last Session the Eastern Province Herald says:—“It is satisfac­tory to
learn that the ineffectiveness of a dismembered opposition has at last
opened the eyes ot the progressive mem­bers of Parliament to the
necessity of organising for combined action. To speak of futile efforts
would, perhaps, unfairly represent the results of the Opposition in
their assaults on the Government. There has been a partial success, for
they have, to some extent, modified the measures of Government, and we
might point to the Additional Railway I Works Bill as a signal instance
of effec­tive criticism by which the amount ot the contemplated loan was
materially re­duced. Opposition success, however, was not due to any
parliamentary tactics, but in spite of the absence of any combined
effort, and may be entirely attributed to the ability of its leaders and
the inherent weakness of the Government, for never since the
introduction of Responsible Government has there been a more miserable
exhibition of feebleness than that displayed day by day by the present
administration. The extraordi­nary policy pursued by Mr. Rhodes in
shunting his friends and forming a Ministry from the ranks of the
Opposi­tion, for a time paralysed public life in the House of Assembly.
The followers— at any rate the thinking portion of them — were perplexed
as to their position, for they had not the same reasons as their leaders
for swallowing their convictions, and many of them simply became the
flotsam and jetsam of the political ocean. But, of course, this could
not last long, they were bound to gravitate towards some centre, and
that centre was found on the Opposition benches. The unpre­cedented
action of Mr. Rhodes has con­sequently had good effect, for Sir Gordon I
Sprigg has carried few of his friends with him. while the three
ex-Ministers have been joined by many who supported them when in office,
he has consequently thrown these two parties into each others arms, and
by their combination we ought to have a formidable opposition when they
are systematically ’ organised and led.”

AT SEA.

NOTHING is clearer from the speeches of the members for

Kingwilliamstown of Monday last than the fact that both members are at
sea in connection with the political status quo. They have been
completely nonplussed by the recent changes in our politics. Both Mr.
WARREN and Mr. SCHERMBRUCKER seem unable to see that there was any
sacrifice on the part of Mr. INNES and his colleagues in relinquishing
office with its emolu­ments as a protest against Sir JAS. SIVEWRIGHT’S
Ministerial actions. So that according to the members for
Kingwilliamstown the three ex-Ministers gave up pUce and power out of
sheer cussedness. Perhaps, by reason of advanced age these gentlemen
have become incapable of dealing with a rapidly changing political
situation; but others may be permitted to believe the statements of the
three ex-Ministers about their inability to work with a colleague whose
blundering dis­position had become chronic. Nor do Messrs. SCHERMBRUCKER
and WARREN discern any irregularity in the action of Sir GORDON SPRIGG
in taking the first available oppor­tunity to take office under a
gentle­man he had a few weeks befpre denounced in unmeasured terms at
East London and Komgha. To many this will appear as a very strange view
of the conduct of Responsible Government.

The worst thing of all revealed by these speeches is the absence of any
indication of any effort to bring about a combination of men in the
House who shall work together, as Mr. HOFMEYR and the Bond have done, to
further the interests of the country. For example Mr. SCIIERM- BRUCKER
favours a diamond tax and an excise ; and so does Mr. WARREN. Now,
unless these gentlemen subject themselves to a certain amount of
discipline, for the purpose of coming to an understanding with others
who believe, more or less, in these measures, although others again may
hold different views on smaller matters to which these members may not
altogether subscribe—unless, in short, they sink minor things, personal
jealousies and grudges, in the desire to co-operate with others, it
cannot be expected these gentlemen can accomplish anything in the
direction of achieving their desires. It is for the electors to teach
them the importance of unit­ing with others who in common with them are
ranged against the methods of the Bond. If they cannot see their way to
do so then the duty of electors is plain—it is to look about for other
representatives.

THE NEW CRUSADE.

WE are gratified, indeed, to
remark that a regular crusade is being begun in various quarters against
the type of candidate which generally enters Parliament boasting about
being independent ” of party. Our contemporary, the Midland News,
concurring with our description of the type as being neither fish nor
fowl, very properly observes, such a member “ ends by sacrificing “
principle to gratifying personal “ pique.” On the urgent importance of
combined action in the proper working of Responsible Government, Mr.
HENRY MAASDORP the very accomplished candidate for Graaff Rcinet, has
spoken well and to the point, and we commend his observations to men
like Mr. SAMPSON, who seem to have a different conception of
Parliamentary duties. Mr. MAASDORP is reported to have said :—He would
tell them that
in forming an organization they “must give up a great deal of what “ was
called independent spirit. One man will say ‘ Oh I I am in-

dependent, I will vote for whomsoever I like.’ In order to carry out
Responsible Government there must be two parties, every member should
remain loyal to his own party. Party Government could not be carried out
under the circumstances unless what he had stated was adhered to. He saw
in Parliament that very disorganization among the Independent party
which he had been complaining of. It would be necessary in the next “
Parliament to have a better defined line of political parties. The Bond,
though well organized, could not form a Government, but by its power it
influenced the Ministry, for the reason that there was no organization
among the “ other party.”

chise Act of last year the

coloured voter was much in evidence
in the politics of this country. We hear little or nothing of him now,
doubtless because the efferversence produced by the Franchise agitation,
has simmered down. It will be in the recollection of the readers of this
journal that while the agitation lasted we urged the coloured
people to reserve their powder for the elections, when theyfcould by
combination help tofreturn to Parliament men favourablefto their cause,
pledged to combine—fas Bondmen have done—to protect, among other things,
the coloured
people against legislation injurious to their interests as a class. The
opportunity of the coloured voter is come ; but we look about for him in
vain, although by a little exertion, he can do for himself lasting
service. There are a considerable number of constituencies in the
Western districts in which, if properly organised, the coloured voter
could do wonders in the direction of helping to put into Parliament
liberal-minded men in the places of the indifferent sort of Bondmen we
have become used to of late years, who hail from these divisions. It
will be a revelation to many to hear that the constituency of
Stellenbosch, famous by
its associations with Mr. HOFMEYR, has 900 coloured voters as against
750 purely Dutch voters. Many other places such as the Paarl, Worcester,
Beaufort West, Oudtshoorn and others, which, although
they have not the preponderance of coloured voters over the Afrikanders,
have, nevertheless, a voting power that is not to be trifled with. Now,
while we would be the last to countenance any reactionary procedure on
the part of the coloured voters to take the representation of these
districts into their hands, yet
we think they would not be misusing their undoubted rights if they
support with their votes men of learning and intelligence in whom the
sense of justice towards all is fully developed, and who would enter the
combination that would
have as one of the planks of its platform resistence of oppression of
the coloured people by reason of the accident of their colour. We think
our political leaders should induce such men to take the field; and no
endeavour spared to get the coloured voters to support them. Where are
the BOWERS the GRENDONS and the ALLYS of the Franchise agitation ? Here
is an opening when they can do their people and the country generally a
practical and lasting service!

Messrs. Warren and Schermbrucker addressed their King Williamstown
con­stituents on Monday evening last. Re­viewing the work of the last
Session Mr. Warren said he did not consider the Logan contract
sufficient reason for breaking the Ministry up, and was still in
ignorance as to the real cause of the disruption. He did not know what
the policy of the present Government was and would wait until it
develops before pledging himself to support it. Referring to Mr.
Merriman’s opposition to the East London Harbour Bill he trusted a man
so inimical to their interests should never be in the Government of the
country. As to the future he was for an excise.
