SAUCE FOR THE GOOSE.

TRUTH is reported to have its residence at the bottom of a well, and it
often appears impossible to discover what is truth because of the foul
condition of the water in that well. We pen this trite ob­servation
having in our minds the charges that are so recklessly made about our
people as registered voters. Some persons, and some of our newspaper
contemporaries will in­sist, and persist in insisting, that Natives go
like sheep to the poll, following the guidance of some “ slim fellow ’
who leads them not so much for their welfare as to secure his own ends.
We have often wondered how such a belief came into existence, but there
is a saying somewhat to this effect : “ A man never looks

behind the door unless he has been in the habit of hiding there
himself,” and now we have it on the highest authority that lead­ing men
like sheep is a labour undertaken by the Africander Bond, that there
need not be any mis­take we give the exact words of the new Bond organ,
Ons Land. Let us first, however, explain how the paragraph came to be
written. For some time this new HOFMEYR organ has been writing to prove
that the Bond was small in numbers and weak in influence, and in one of
the last issues the Bond and the Legislative Council was the theme, and
from that article we take the following bold and outspoken state­ment :—

We all know that the Legislative Coun­cil counts a great many more
Bondsmen on its benches. This was, of course, to be expected, for even
if all Bondsmen are not qualified voters there are, on the other hand, a
great many among them worth a great deal more than their indi­vidual
votes—men of influence, action, energy, and organizing power, who assert
themselves both before and on the polling day and know how to gain votes
of non-Bondsmen for the cause. The triumph of the National party depends
on the efforts of such men more than on anything else.

This is letting the cat out of the bag, indeed ; and it reminds us of
the man who to avoid arrest went crying “ Stop thief.” The very people
who have most loudly de­nounced the Missionaries, and the so called
“negropholists” for ad­vising the Natives how to vote, now boast that
they do so themselves and are triumphing in consequence of such action

Strange to say this boast of lead­ing men like sheep is not confined to
the Bond, for at a Licensed Victuallers Association meeting held in
Capetown recently a Mr DAY spoke and said: “ The Li­censed Victuallers
of this city had no idea of their own strength at elections, and he
could assure them that with the support of the trade he would run any
man in the Council with confidence and suc­cess.”

We are glad to have these state­ments on the eve of Parliament meeting
to discuss the Franchise,

because if the Natives are in­fluenced their friends only do what the
Bond and the Licensed Vic­tuallers approve of. But we main­tain that our
people are not led in that way. They do not vote for “ a glass ” or to
give power to the Africander Bond, but as wise men to advance their own
interests.

A trip to Capetown through the Western Districts affords the person
undertaking it an opport unity of making observations of no
inconsiderable in­terest. Not the least among these is that relating to
the attitude of the nationalities inhabiting the Colony to­wards each
other. The Western districts of the Colony are peopled mostly, as is
well known, by Colonists of Dutch ex­traction. This section of the
community is generally regarded as being unfriendly disposed towards the
Natives; and, no doubt, the impression is strengthened rather than
weakened by the tone of the baser sort of Afrikander journalism which
seems never more delighted than when it has a jibe or any hard thing to
say of the Native. But repeated visits to the West, both by sea and by
land, has deepened the conviction that if the representatives of
Afrikanderism in the Press and the Senate are to be seriously taken in
the expressions of their senti­ments about the aboriginal Natives, then
they either cruelly libel a people well-disposed towards our countrymen
by showing their proteges as being ini- mically inclined, or they must
stand convicted of being grossly ignorant of the real feelings of the
Dutch people. Otherwise, if all that appears in these prints about the “
Naturlen ” is a faithful representation of the opinions cherished by the
Dutch towards the Natives, all that can be said is that then the bark of
the Afrikanders is more to be feared than their bite. Passing through
these regions more than once, the present write.’ has been struck by the
perfect temper and respectful bear­ing extended to his party and his
colour by Dutch colonists. It was impossible to forbear contrasting it
with the beha- viour of this class towards our people in the East, where
frowns and grimaces are the most that a Native can expect from his white
neighbours. It is not so in the West. As Natives we feel at home with
white colonists ; and such a condition of affairs tends more than
any­thing else to promote concord and a good understanding between
races, to the mutual advantage of all. If the Dutch Press were but to
strive to mirror this aspect of the relations of the section of colonist
its essays represent, it would render no mean service to the state as a
whole, and would ??? the lime when useless and senseless bickerings over
the mere pigment of a persons skin would come to an end, and all devote
themselves, each in their relative spheres, to the earnest development
of the large resources of this country, which we ail with to see take
its place among the countries of the world.
