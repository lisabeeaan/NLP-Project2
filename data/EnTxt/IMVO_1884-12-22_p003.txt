NATIVE OPINION

LOYALTY

HE is wilfully blind indeed who either denies or ignores the fact that
the loyalty of all the in-habitants of this southern portion of the Dark
Continent to England’s QUEEN is just now put upon itsmettle. When
loud-mouthed tall of a Flag, other than the QUEEN’S has proceeded from a
certain section of the inhabitants of South Africa which is known to
possess, through some freak of fortune, political power; and menaces to
interrupt the march of the QUEEN’S troops— for what purpose no
matter—within Her Majesty’s dominions; and sympathy openly avowed with
the trampling under foot of engage- ments solemnly entered into by the
QUEEN and other States—when all these, capped by seditious writings and
speeches, exist within the Colony, we say, it is absurd to blame those
in Great Britain, at least, for arriving at the conclusion that our
loyalty out here is, at all events, considerably strained. Under the
circumstances it became incumbent upon those who honestly believed, but
had meantime been silent, that the susbsitution of another Flag for that
of the united Kingdom, was prejudical our best to interest, to make
their voice heard on behalf of their side of the question : and the
medium they chose was the establish- ment of the Empire League, which,
while abstaining front directly tam- pering with the aspirations of
politi- cal parties within the Colony jealously guards the ties which
bind us to the illustrious Empire “ over which the sun never sets.”
Setting aside the untoward differences be- tween the Government and the
Natives in the past, chiefly owing to local parties turning Natives into
a political shibboleth, we may say Natives, as a mass, have ever been
attached to the just and humane Government of the QUEEN ; and their
loyalty to her Throne is, as was aptly put by a Natal comtem- porary, a
passion and a principle. If we were to express the genuine feeling of
our people as a whole they would exceedingly rejoice if the various
forms of gov- ernment now obtaining in South Africa were swept away
to-morrow”, and the direct sway of her Majesty’s Government put in their
place. This being the case, then, we see no reason why our people should
not make their voice heard in favour of the return of British influence
to these distracted regions, and wish- ing that it may at least
continue, at any rate, in those portions of South Africa which are at
present pre- cluded from enjoying the privi- leges of Responsible
Government. Now is the time for the Natives, when the QUEEN is near, to
ask her to remain. She is the only friend of the weak and oppressed
among all the mighty powers across the sea. Meetings of the League
should be formed among our people too, and that without delay ; and
reso­lutions sent straight to our good Governor, Sir HERCULES ROBINSON
for transmission to the QUEEN. Such a course of procedure would re­dound
to the lasting credit of the Aboriginies of South Africa. Let the
educated and intelligent Natives lead the way.

A MEETING of the Board of Revisers of the Kafir Bible brought its
labours to a conclusion in this town on Saturday week The Board
consisted of the Rev. John A Chalmers (Graham’s Town), Rev. Bryce Ross
(Pirie), and Rev. A. Kropf (Stutter- heim). The New Testament of the
Board presented to the Native public some years ago was welcomed in
certain quarters with that prejudice which seems to be reserved for
every work of its kind in all Christian nations; and since the very warm
discussions on the merits or de- merits of the Board Revision. Kafir
Bible readers have been driven into two hostile camps viz.., one
composed of admirers of the old, and the other of those of the new.
Under the circumstances the Home Foreign Bible Society has decided, we
understand, to hold out the olive branch to the two parties by
appointing a final Board to go through the work as revised, the results
of the labours of which they will regard as the standard translation.
All the expenses being defrayed by the Society.
