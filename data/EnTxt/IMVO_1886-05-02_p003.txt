There is, however, a fatal objection which we wish to point out to
our Tembuland friends. We are
told that they have secured the
gratis services of a young European
gentleman, who was formerly in
the Government service, to manage
the thing for them. We happen
to know that the name of this gentleman has been for some time past
erased from the good books of the
Cape Government, and the young
man himself is looked upon with,
unkindly eyes in official circles.
Now, we warn the people against
employing men of this kind in
these great matters. Their very
appearance in connection with these
things goes far to prejudice otherwise good causes. In the eyes of

the official circle they play the part
that the dead fly plays in the ointment. Many a plausible Native
cause has been wrecked by the
hollow advocacy of white men who,
having got into scrapes, and found
it inconvenient to remain among
their people, have betaken themselves to Native chiefs. These men
essay, and are even trusted, to be
the peoples’ mouthpiece in matters
concerning the very existence of
Native nations. Our own experience
under Government is that the
grievances of Natives would receive
better consideration if urged by
Natives themselves either through
Magistrates, or through Native
young men who possess the requisite
ability and standing to urge these.
The undoubted friends of the
Natives among the white people
may be easily consulted when necessary, but the damnable habit of
making use of persons of no standing, simply because they possess a
white skin, will yet engineer the
Native tribes into inextricable
difficulties.

THE Rev. John Walton, M.A., who has,
for the last eight years with commanding
dignity, filled the position of General
Superintendent of Wesleyan Missions,
supervised from Graham’s Town,
preached his farewell sermon in Commemoration Church, Graham’s Town, on
Sunday last, and will be leaving for
England in the course of this week. In
his departure the Wesleyan Church will
lose a very eloquent preacher, a shrewd
and capable ecclesiastical statesman, and
the Native people will miss the influence
of a kind and fearless champion of their
rights as British citizens. With his extensive experience of Native
progress in
India, which is a good way in advance of
ours out here,Mr. Walton has sympathised
with the intellectual and social improvement of the Natives by education
and it
is a thousand pities that in these critical
times it has become impossible for him to
remain among us. We wish the rev.
gentleman and his a safe voyage to old
England.

the Missionary Institution Lovedale,

year—a position which is very creditable
to himself and to the Institution from
which he hails. Master Bennie is son of
the late Mr. J. A. Bennie, some time
Boarding Master at Lovedale. Last year
he headed the First Class in the Elementary Examination. The Honours
Examination is no ordinary test, demanding as it does a thorough
knowledge
of the rudiments and something more
than the rudiments of the subjects that
are employed to cultivate the intellectual
faculties of a man—Classics and Science.
We are satisfied Master Bennie will yet
give a good account of himself in the
more showing University Examinations.

THE Journal of Friday last has a very exhaustive article on the subject
of the Franchise. It corrects the universal though false view that “
wealth gives a title to a preponderant share in the choice of
representatives,”‘and urges that “the stake which one man has in the
country, (supposing him to be not a mere bird of passage) is about as
great as an- other's.” “ It is,” adds our contemporary, “ the interest
of his life, freedom, family, property—his all, in fact; which is as
much to the poor man as the rich man’s all is to him.” Hence England and
America have resorted to almost universal suffrage. The Journal again
asks the questions, which have been often asked and as often slurred
over—“ have the natives shown themselves unfit for a fair share of
voting power ? ” “ Have they proved themselves unfit to judge of good
candidates, or shown a tendency to send up dangerous men? ” Nothing of
the sort. The natives are to be crushed because their sympathies are
English and Imperialistic; and in the ensuing struggle between English
freedom, progress, and enterprise and Afrikander indolence, prejudice
and retrogression the natives are sure to side with the English.
