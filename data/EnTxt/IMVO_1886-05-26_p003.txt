DEAR SIR,—Will you kindly allow me to make known to the readers of your
paper that at the Wesleyan Methodist Conference recently held at Queen’s
Town it was resolved that the Hymn Book now in use in our Native
Churches shall be revised and enlarged, and that the committee appointed
to carry out this work will be thankful to receive suggestions and help
from any of our Ministers and people who are interested in this
important undertaking. Opinions are solicited on the omissions that
should be made in the present Collection of Hymns, and suggestions with
regard to additions that might be made from Hymn Books used in other
churches. Original Compositions and Translations will be specially
prized, and any assist­ance that can be afforded will be highly
appreciated.

In order to get this work well in hand by the next Conference, it is
hoped that the Committee will be favoured with communications at an
early date.

I shall be glad to receive and acknow­ledge all letters on this subject.

  I am, &c., WILLIAM J. HACKER, Convener of the Committee.

ONLY the other day we pointed out in

these columns the serious difficulties the natives may find
themselves in by allowing themselves to be led by the nose by any
white man, or set of white men, in

matters relating to the protection
and maintenance of their rights and
privileges under Government. We
urged upon them the absolute necessity of carefully examining whatever
proposals might be made to
them by this class of men, and that
if needful such scrutiny should be
extended to the men themselves.
“ Men, not Measures ” should in
their present circumstances be their
motto, for often have native chiefs
suffered injustice at the hands of
Government simply from the fact
that they conducted their cases
through discredited agents. Thus,
we are more sanguine of the

We are led into these remarks
by a resolution passed at a meeting
of the Tsomo Traders’ Association,
giving approval to the Transkeian
Representation Bill. The Tsomo
Traders claim that in their approval
of this measure they are joined by
native chiefs and headmen, who
have sent numerously-signed petitions in its favour “ to prove that
“ the measure is highly esteemed ”
(we quote from the report) by
“ the natives.” If the chiefs and
headmen have actually done what
they are here reported to have done,
then we have no hesitation in saying
they have been most grossly and
wofully deceived by the Traders
who have the temerity to arrogate
to themselves the sole right of
expressing an opinion on these
matters when they say in one of
their resolutions;—‘‘ This Associa-
“ tion is further of opinion that no
“ gentlemen in the Colony are so
“ well qualified to express an opinion
“ upon the subject as those living
“ always and entirely in the midst
“ of and in daily intercourse with
“ the natives of these territories.”
The Transkeian Representation Bill
that has been submitted for the
approval of Parliament by Mr.
UPINGTON is so shockingly bad that
we cannot understand how any
number of sensible gentlemen can
approve of it. No one who knows
the length, breadth, and wealth of

the Transkeian Territories can for a
moment contend that two members
are what the just interests of that
quarter of the Colony claim. Do
these Chiefs and Headmen approve
of this? Then there is the extremely
flagitious distinction which it is
sought to legalize by the Bill that
because a man has been clothed
with a black skin by his Creator he
should be possessed of landed property to the amount of £500, while
his white-skinned neighbour should
only be worth £25 to be qualified to
have a voice in the making of laws
by which he is to be governed ! Do
these Chiefs and Headmen approve
of this also? The Bill proposes
that all black men within the large
area bounded by the Kei and the
Umzimkulu, and by the Kwahlamba
Range and the sea, should be
grouped and have a Council which
would elect one representative from
among Hut-Tax payers to be the

spokesmen of all the native races
within this large tract of country!
The thing may at once be dismissed
as a burlesque were it not that those recommending it were coaxing it
forward as a serious proposal.
This grouping of natives is done
professedly with the object of circumscribing the influence of the
natives in Parliament; and herein
the monstrosity of the action
of the Chiefs and Headmen who
have signed the suicidal petition comes out in a very striking
degree. Surely these Chiefs
and Headmen must be veritable
modern HAMANS to thus erect gallows on which they might themselves be
suspended. Need we add
one more straw to the load of guilt
and infamy that these Chiefs and
Headmen must now feel? We
trow not. But no time should be
lost by the more reasonable Chiefs,
Headmen, and people to protest
against the vicious principles that
are being introduced into the law

and social order of the country by
this Bill. If the Territories are
to have representation, let it be
equitable and honest, and let the
Franchise be the same in the

Trans- as in the Cis-Kei.

WE are indebted to the courtesy of the
Superintendent-General of Education for
the interesting yearly Report on the state
of Education laid before Parliament
which we hope soon to notice at some
length. The striking feature in this year’s
volume is the inclusion of the Inspectors’
Reports on the state of education in their
various circuits. These afford increased
interest to a work which has heretofore
specialised itself by mere figures. We
are pleased to observe that notwithstanding the untoward times through
which
the country has had to pass, the coach of
education, with its genial Jehu in the
person of Dr. Dale, has not suffered considerable reverses, and that it
is still
going on, imparting light and sweetness
in its course.

A SUGGESTION has been made in our Kafir columns by a Native
correspondent that a monster memorial should be made to His Excellency
the Governor as the Queen’s representative, for the release of the Kafir
Chiefs and other prisoners of war now confined in the Batteries at Cape
Town. The grounds on which this course is urged are those given in Holy
Writ, Leviticus x:xv. 10, “And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and
proclaim liberty throughout the land unto all the inhabi­tants thereof:
it shall be a jubilee unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his
family.” This suggestion is made in view of the Jubilee of Her Majesty
the Queen, which will be cele­brated next year. We have an impres­sion,
though we cannot quote chapter and verse for it at the moment, that
prisoners were let free on the occasion of the Jubi­lee of George III in
July, 1809. In any case the idea is a good one, and we trust it will at
least obtain the moral support of the Colonists. If Kreli and Umditshwa
(before he died) could be peaceably set­tled, surely the magnanimity of
our Government may be extended to the wretches now on the Table Bay
Harbour Works.

THE Education Estimates have been
engineered through the Assembly. The
teachers’ salaries are, according to Mr.
Tudhope, to have the principle of per-
centage reduction applied to them. We
only hope this will not extend to the
miserable pittances, ranging from £30 to
£50 per annum. For those who would
apply retrenchment so far would be liable
to Mr. Sauer’s Cruelty to Animals’ Act.
The Estimates had, however, to go
through the ordeal of Bond bitter and
biting criticism. Mr. Le Roex set the
ball rolling. He moved that £8,458 spent
on Industrial Schools for Natives be
struck out, for he could not see why white
people should pay for the education of
Natives, quite overlooking the fact that
Natives pay taxes too. Then the merits
of educated and uneducated Natives as
servants came under review, but from
practical experience Mr. Dyer disposed of
the absurd contention that the more a
Native man was educated the more
roguish he became. From these
yearly declamations in the Assembly our
people should learn to refute the
declaimers by deeds.
