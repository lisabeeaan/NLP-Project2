  WE sincerely congratulate Mr.
  CHAS. MOLTENO on his

return for Tembuland. Uis elec-
tion represents one of the most
remarkable episodes of the present
General Election. The campaign
in Tembuland was begun months
ago—long before even the as-
sembling of the last Session ; con-
sequently the constituency was
thoroughly canvassed when Mr.
MOLTENO’S candidature was an-
nounced three or four weeks back,
and was prosecuted with such
vigour and courage amidst showers
of inordinate abuse and misrepre-
sentation with the result now
announced. This result will be
admitted by every fair-minded man
to be a credit to the independence of
the Native voter. When Colonel
GRIFFITH dissociated himself from
the Party which has made itself a
name for advocating justice to
Natives; and Messrs. V. SAMPSON
and T. A. KING were trying to sail
in as candidates independent of
Party, the Natives refused to pledge
themselves to such Wobblers,
knowing as they did that under our
system of government nothing can
be done except by and through
party. Mr. MOLTENO came forward
boldly avowing that he was tho-
roughly in sympathy with the Party
in which the Natives place full
confidence, and promising to fulfil
his pledges by and through the
counsels of the Party they knew to
bo friendly to them. The result
was that he was gladly received by
this section of voters. Mr. SAMP-
SON seeing this at once had recourse
to that most vicious expedient
of setting the Natives and the
Europeans by the ears, as if the

interests of these two classes in Tembuland were not one and the same.
We are glad that this silly device has signally failed. We are well
aware that many men dull of intellect have bitterly assailed those who
have worked to secure Mr. MOLTENO’S return. They have been represented
as the enemies of the Border for putting forward a W estern man ; and
the Natives have been called names for favouring him. Now, what are the
facts ? Let us state once for all that no Native would support a man
that they believed to be an enemy of the Border, and no one has as yet
attempted to show Mr. MOLTENO to be such. But, it has been said that he
was allied to a Party inimically disposed to the Border. Fortunately for
our people Parties in Parliament are not formed on territorial lines ;
and it can be shown by facts which cannot be shaken that the men who
have actually opened the Border Port are those of the Party with which
Mr. MOLTENO is identified, while others made a profession and did
nothing. It was the MOLTENO Mi­nistry—a so-called Western Ministry
—which commenced the Railway from East London to Queenstown. This
Government was succeeded by the first SPRIGG Ministry, and its record is
nowhere. The SCANLEN Government came in, and extended the line to Aliwal
North. In both these Cabinets Mr. MERRIMAN was the Commissioner of
Public Works and had the direct responsibility of carrying through
Parliament these great measures for Border progress. The second SPRIGG
Government did nothing. The first RHODES Ministry afterwards, through
Mr. SAUER, engineered- the Bethulie junction. These work?, which have
really opened East London, were worth millions, while all Mr.
SCHERM­BRUCKER can show for his friendship is the dredger, worth two or
three thousand pounds. Who maintains in the face of such facts that
those favoured by Natives are enemies of the Frontier while their
opponents are the only friends? As a fact Mr. RHODES and Sir J
SIVEWRIGHT, of the party which some profess to favour, are the men who
have sought to tare up the rails of the Border line. The attempt to form
Parties on a territorial basis is foolish and absurd. Natives recognise
this. They knew also that Parties are based on them. It is right and
proper that with their votes they should assist those who will work with
their friends to safeguard their interests. Any reasonable man will see
that the representative of so vast a Native district, like Tembuland,
should enjoy the entire confidence of the Natives, and Mr. MOLTENO, of
his rivals being the only gentle­man who possesses this essential
qualification, is the right man in the right place. Then, it has been
compk ined that he is a comparative­ly unknown man, knowing nothing of
the wants ot the Natives, brought forward to displaca Col. GRIFFITH who
knows the people well. In reply we may say that Mr. MOLTENO as Private
Secretary to his late father when that gentleman ruled the Colony
acquired a great knowledge of the Natives and the Native question.
Besides he has been in sympathy and in close touch all these years with
the gen­tleman who have earned for them­selves the title of lovers of
the Natives Natives know people by the company they keep, not so much by
what they profess, and Mr. MOLTENO’S professions corres­ponding with his
actions in regard to Party association proved conclusive. From long
experience our people know that those who had never seen them have done
them greaterand more lasting benefit than those who pretend to know them
but whose hearts are not right. It is the heart that must be right. As
to a member knowing the wants of his constituents, it may be relied upon
that of these he would be in­formed in due course ; and no member can
profess to know every­thing about every corner of his constituency. He
can be kept in­formed cf wants and it would suffice. Anove all Mr. CHAS.
MOLTENO is a gentleman imbued with a great fund of common sense, who
understands human nature; and is an able and eloquent speaker wfithal.
By him the interests of Tembuland will bo efficiently looked after in
the House; and we congratulate him and the con­stituency which has
elected him on the result; believing as we do that in honouring him the
con­stituency has honoured itself.

THE KING CONTEST.

THE contest for the two seats 1 for Kingwilliamstown has resulted in a
victory for the Wob­blers—the old members (Messrs

SCHERMBRUCKER and WARREN), who belong neither to the Opposi­tion nor to
the Bond Party—the latter owns the Government—being returned. They will
themselves admit that Mr. GEORGE HAY has engaged them in a pretty smart
fight; and that although he has failed to win the seat, yet he is not
discredited, distanced as he is by Mr. WARREN by merely two hun­dred
votes on a Register of three thousand voters. This fact alone is a
sufficient warrant for his confidence in coming forward. Moreover, his
opponents who at first thought his was a bogus candidature, without a a
backing on the part of the elect­ors, must now, if fair, admit that they
had misread the signs; more especially as they have had to resort to
every unfair expedients, in addition to the hardest fighting to keep out
a single-handed candidate. It seems now clear that had Mr. HAY secured a
colleague to run with him to secure the second votes of his supporters
which have gone to swell the totals of his adversaries, he would have
probably wen the two seats. His poll how­ever, will now serve to
encourage him to work steadily so as to achieve victory on the next
appeal. There can be no doubt about it that Mr. GEORGE HAY has on the
pre­sent occasion lost simply and solely through the desertion at the
last moment of a large number of European voters in town who had
promised him support. It is not the first, nor the second, nor probably
the third time that some European voters of the town of Kingwilliamstown
have acted in a manner reminding one of a swarm of locusts, driven
hither and thither by every gust of wind. On this occasion mere noise
from the Licensed Victuallers quarters has fright­ened them away from a
laud­able resolve to put Mr. HAY in. It was known for weeks that in town
Mr. WAEREN was the weakest candidate, whatever he might be in the
country among his bucolic friends. Everybody acknow­ledged that there
was no comparison between Mr. GEORGE HAY and Mr. WARREN in so far as
fitness for political service is concerned. Mr. J HAY therefore deserved
better support from intelligent voters; but, as usual, it remained for
our Native people to indicate by their votes the man who really would do
the constituency the greatest credit in the House and in the country;
and Kingwilliamstown has, through the action of Europeans, missed the
opportunity of materially strength­ening its representation in the eyes
of the general public by returning a capable young politician of whose
appearance nobody would be ashamed. Of Mr. WARREN this, of course,
cannot be said, but through the deliberate action of many of our
European friends, whose views as to the requirements of the country seem
very circum­scribed, a gentleman, dull of in­tellect, who has, in
addition played the political rat, goes down for the third time as about
the smartest politician Kingwilliams­town can contribute to the
intelli­gence of the House ! Mr. WARREN has however led Mr. HAY in the
town poll, not because the latter was less popular, but because, after
Mr. HAY’S great speech on Nomi­nation Day ^the Colonel and his confreres
resorted to the despicable trick of fanning the smouldering embers of
race prejudice. TITUS OATES of old plied long and success­fully h’s
business of manufacturing Catholic plots for the behoof of a credulous
pubb’c whenever times seemed dull in the days of the Stuarts. Mr.
SCHERMBRUCKER, well aware of the credulity of the average European
elector in this constituency, has made the most of his opportunities to
set the whites against the blacks in this election. We have not thought
it worth while to notice the Colonel’s blatant nonsense about the
election here and in Tembuland as we did not think the European public
so gullible as to be taken in by strategems so thinly veiled, but the
result shows that the attempt which we thought would be deemed pure
election burlesque by discriminating voters has proved successful to the
extent of causing a number of voters to drop Mr. HAY and vote for Mr.
WARREN, from a fear that the blacks, as represented by this mis­chievous
politician, really meant to do the whites harm. Of course nothing of the
kind has been thought of, and it is only the pro­duct of the fertile
imagination of the Colonel. For the Native voters we may say they have
nootherdesiro except to place the representation of constituencies in
strong, able men, determined to act fairly by all sections ; and the
type of mem­bers they have in the past assisted in returning proves this
beyond e. doubt.
