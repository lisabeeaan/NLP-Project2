  this Colony in his capacity as managing director of the British South
  Africa Company ; and we are aware that there are those who would
  rejoice to witness similar tactics pursued in respect of the Pondos.
  That unfortunately the wishes of the people who would like to see the
  Colonyengag? in a war with the Pondos are likely to be realised is

  his recent address at East London; then from a casual report that

  Pondoland; although another merely says they are to enable the Cape
  Mounted Rifles to acquire some knowledge of their use.What is more,
  however, there appears an article in the Star which is one of the
  newspapers to which people are wont to look for what may for the time
  being be passing in Mr. RnqpEs’s mind in respect of public affairs. In
  the face of these facts the question of Pondoland becomes one of
  practical politics. “ Internecine warfare, constant disorders, “
  nameless barbarities consequent
  “ upon ‘smellingout’ expeditions” are being put forward by Mr.
  RHODES’S Johannesburg organ as justification for the threatened
  movement against the Pondos. We are not of those who say that at this
  time of day and in full sight of the civilised world the Pondos may go
  on with the outrages comprised in the Star's catalogue without check
  or challenge, on the principle that they are stewing in their own
  juice. We rather incline to the view of our contemporary that “
  practically the only claim “ which the chiefs both in East and ” West
  Pondoland had to their “ independence was contingent upon “ the
  maintenance of the public “ peace.” But what we fail to understand is
  for those who, with, the word peace and goodwill on their lips,
  proceed to bring down maxim guns to bear on the Pondos to do the very
  thing they are deprecating in Pondoland. If the Pondos had been
  definitely told to desist from certain things repugnant to a strong
  civilized community on its borders, and they persisted, then we could
  understand the bellicose movement; but seeing this has not been done,
  it is clear to us that the same policy is to be
  pursued towards them which was carried out towards LOBENGULA— who
  without due warning was pounced upon for indulging in a
  time-honoured practice of punishing the Mashonas for occasional
