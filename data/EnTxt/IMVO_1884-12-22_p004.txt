OUR observations on stock-stealing would seem to have left an impression
we did not desire that they should convey on the minds of some of our
English contemporaries. We do not deem it our call to run after every
thing that may be said about us but when the Cape Argus, one of the
organs by which we swear, refers our remarks as follows, we feel bound
to make an explanation. Says our contemporary “Something, too, might be
done from the side of the well-disposed natives A capital article in the
only native journal we have in this country recently showed how little
tolerance honest natives were disposed to extend to the proceedings
which give their whole race bad name. It appeared as if the writer were
half inclined to favour capital Punishment as the penalty for hardened
(offenders.” Now, capital punishment ap­peared in our article in
connection with the native way of proceeding towards thieves, to show
that thieving was never Countenanced by the natives as a com­munity. It
is too late in the day even for the natives to hang people for stealing,
We agree with our contemporary that Something might be done from the
side of the well-disposed natives.

OPINIONS OF THE PRESS.

The following are further expressions of press opinions respecting
ourselves which we were not able to reproduce with the others.

In our last issue we mentioned the launch of the Imvo Zabantsundu, which
it may be necessary to explain to those who may not have seen our former
brief notice is not a new tug, or surfboat, or even a vessel of
any-kind. It is a new Kafir weekly newspaper that has been started in
King William's Town, and it suc­cessive numbers are equal to the one
before us it is destined to be the medium of much good. The number of
Kafirs who can read and write their own lan­guage well, and English
creditably, is steadily increasing year by year, and Imvo Zabantsundu
will accelerate the desire of the Kafir for sufficient education to
enable him to read it. The editor gives much good advice to his fellow
Kafirs in the event of a small-pox scare—so much dreaded in Tembuland.
He says: “We urge our readers, especially those of our colour, to keep
very cool in the face of small-pox. Nothing, we are told, is more
favourable to its propagation than panic. The people will needs cleanse
their homes and themselves and those who had not been vaccinated by all
means do so at once ” This is such excellent advice that we urge its
adoption even without the stimulus of a small-pox scare. Jabavu is quite
an enthusiast in cricket—-a game which seems quite to hit the Kafir
fancy. He says “the very motives which beget an innocent love for
cricket in the white man act in precisely the same way in the native.
Believing thoroughly in this doctrine ourselves we should not wonder to
hear some day that some native eleven, consisting of those who may
distinguish themselves in the proposed tournament (at King William's
Town) are being equipped for a cricket campaign in England." When we say
that the leading article closes with the following exhortation we are
sure the Imvo will receive patronage of many good people of all classes:
"the whole of the native need to be stimulated to industry and to
following scientific methods of farming and we will always try to make
this paper a means for educating the Native Farmers. We shall do all in
our power to suggest practial methods for the solution of the various
social problems confronting the Natives, and the first and foremost
among these may be placed in the Liquor Question. "
