HERCULES ROBINSON — SIR GEO. CATHCART — in the name of the Queen. The
case of the Glen Grey spoliation is most indefen­sible, and this being
the position the Governor could have without difficulty excercised his
power of veto over the decisions of a party ministry with beneficial
results. In the first place, the transaction is illegal. An act of
Parliament should have been passed first to set aside the award of Sir
GEORGE CATHCART, the act would then have been set apart for the
signification of Her Majesty’s pleasure. As it is what has been done
will be con­sidered by all except the free-booters themselves or their
accomplices as a barefaced act of public plunder.

Then the Pondo question is clearly within the compass of the duties of
the High Commissioner. Pondoland is an independent state. It is not
annexed to the Colony even—which might have given the Cape Govern­ment
some show of reason to inter­fere in its affairs. Why under the
cir­cumstances Sir HERCULES ROBINSON should consider him self bound by
the advice of the Cape Ministry will puzzle the most consumate
constitu­tional lawyer in the land. Such, however, is the case; and the
Pondos having lost all confidence even in the Queen’s representative at
Cape Town, are now attempting to climb up some other way to reach the
Queen’s ear. We are satisfied our present Governor would settle the
question without delay and satisfac­torily to all concerned if he could
only be induced to lend the country his experienced judgment. Is it too
late ?
