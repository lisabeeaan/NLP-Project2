AT Cradock on Wednesday last the Secretary for Native Affairs was
inter­viewed by Dr. Ireland and Rev. C. Denyer on behalf of the Town
Council on the subject of the brewing and selling of Kafir beer in the
Native location of Cradock. It would appear that a request made by the
Town Council to the Gov­ernment to promulgate a new Regulation to deal
with this subject was declined by the Government. to reply Mr. Faure
said he was surprised that the regulation had not been approved of as he
had found that wherever he went in the Transkei, the Natives were all
anxious to have as many obstacles as possible put in the way of their
obtaining drink. We are gratified that the honourable gentle­man has
been impressed by this attitude of the Natives, also to see that he is
ready to consult their wishes in every reasonable manner.

MANY will be surprised to learn that the question of the occupation of
lands Glen Grey by Natives is still deemed open by Government, for what
else can be made of the intimation that they and going to inquire into
the matter by Com mission. In the Session of 1889 the feeling of the
country in general was faithfully represented by the present
Commissioner of Crown Lands and Pub lie Works when the subject was
brough before the House by Mr. Joubert, and Mr. Sivewright said he had
thought the House would have passed over the matte in solemn silence and
proceeded to the vote. As a matter of fact then the House gave a
decisive vote against the proposal aimed at dealing with the subject as
ai open one. It puzzles us now to understand under what powers it is
being inquired into, after the express vote of the House against such
inquiry last year.
