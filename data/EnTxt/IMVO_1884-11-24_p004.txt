OUR people are deeply indebted to this very ably conducted paper for the
manner in which it has taken up a question which is, at all events, of
such vital importance to them And as far “ as reflecting the views Of
the natives ” is concern we deem it needless to assure our white friends
that we mean faithfully to follow the lines chalked out in our
introductory article in putting aside “ strong individual impressions to
reflect Native opinion on the questions agi- tating the day. In pursuing
this course in its integrity we do not ol course expect to please all.
In returning to this matter of the arrear taxes we may say there does
exist among our people a strong desire to pay these taxes to Government;
and we are only astonished at the apparent inability of gov­ernment to
comprehend the fact that the people are destitute, and that to squeeze
them at this period of the year and the manner threatened is to drive
them to law­lessness which is the concomitant of poverty and distress,”
and which “will not fail to produce a plentiful crop of thefts and other
crimes.”

IT is gratifying to learn from one of our colour that there is no
stock-stealing in the Cala District, which is, of course, under our
friend Mr. Levey. “The farmers on that
border,” our correspondent—who writes in English—tells us, “ rarely
suffer from theft by the Cala natives.” He goes on to say, there is not
a district in South Africa
where less crime is committed than in Mr. Levey’s, nor anywhere is there
more substantial improvement.” Our correspondent traces this desirable
state of matters to the
absence of canteens, a fact which he italicezes He deprecates, however,

high
amongst the farmers all (his are the italics again) Natives on account

BE it remembered that these remarks were penned, simultaneously with our
leader on ‘‘Stock-theft,” far away in the heart of Tembuland, over a
hundred miles from our Editorial office! The “enemy” has extracted a
joke from the title of the paper Native Opinion, and represented it as
an individual’s opinion. The almost literal agreement of the sentiments
expressed in our leader and those of our correspondent ought to be
sufficient to counteract the machinations of the “ enemy”
in unprejudiced minds. Admirer’s of Mr. Levey’s manner of dealing with
our people that we are, we fail, however, to see what “system” he could
“devise” for the repression of stealing unless he be asked, and he
himself consent, to become a Cabinet Minister to extend his beneficent
policy in Native affairs to the whole of the Frontier and Transkeian
Natives. But even were this, which is mere poetry, to take place, he
would have uphill work to convince his colleagues to allow him a free
kick of the
“ball”; and the Colony, which would not allow carte blanche to a Chinese
Gordon to solve a Basuto difficulty, is not likely to concede that to a
Levey. We have not the
Blue Book before us, but we have an impression Mr. Levey has, in his
reports, devised schemes for the amelioration of the Native races which
would have placed them above the atmosphere of coveting their
neighbours’ stock.
