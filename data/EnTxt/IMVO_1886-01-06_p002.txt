  NATIVE OPINION

  STRAINING AT A GNAT.

A BUTTERWORTH correspondent, writing to the Cape Argus, says that, a
circular “ has been sent to the Missionary

“Superintendents, proposing a reduction of the salaries of Native “
Teachers by twenty per cent. The “amount,” observes the writer, “
hitherto paid has really been too “ small. Now those who receive “ £20
and £30 per annum are to “ be paid £16 and £24 respectively.

This reduction,” the correspond- ent adds, “ will very much retard “ the
work of education, as teachers “ can obtain better pay as policemen “
and clerks. The consequence will “ be that the more efficient men “ will
seek employment elsewhere.”

If what is stated in the remarks we have reproduced has to be attributed
to the retrenchment propaganda, then it is time people opened their eyes
to watch with care and patience its development. This circular reminds
us of the unso- phisticated people of old who were capable of readily
swallowing a quadruped as large as the camel, while they would pull wry
faces and look awfully gruesome over the comparatively insignificant
gnat. Teachers, and Native Teachers of all Teachers, are a notoriously
un- derpaid set of men. This has been the case in ancient times. . OVID,
in one of his most exquisite songs, thus urges school-masters to give
homage to the Etrurian deity MINERVA, the goddess of Litera- ture and
