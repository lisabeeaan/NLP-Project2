Native Opinion,

WE are glad to be able to report a distinct step in the right direction
in connection with the discussion which has taken up much of our space
these past few weeks. In the first place a kind letter has been received
from His Excellency the Governor on the subject, and we have great
pleasure in giving it to the public. It is as follows :—

SIR,—I am desired by His Excellency the Governor to acknowledge the
receipt of the Petition of the Aboriginal Inhabitants of
Kingwilliamstown with reference to the appointment of Location
Inspectors in that Division, and to inform you that His Excellency has
forwarded the ; Petition to his Ministers, from whom it will doubtless
receive careful consideration.

  I am, Sir, Yours faithfully.

It will be remembered that the sorest point with our people in the whole
business was the speed with which the matter was being rushed through
notwith­standing the fact that they had certain representations to '
make. They had had bitter experience of the working of the system
before, from which they gathered that, while it subjected them to
endless worry it was accompanied by no compensating good either to them
or to the public in general. It was perfectly natural therefore that
their feelings ₍ should rebel against the system. It has been said that
similar measures are carried out elsewhere, and Natives have not
complained. We have made inquiries into this statement and find that the
law in these districts is a dead letter—all the harassing accessories of
regis­tration and compulsory branding of Natives’ stock having, in most
places, been dropped many years ago; and people have remained in peace
under the so called Inspectors pretty much as Natives in this division
have done. All the In­spectors do is, at stated intervals, to draw their
salaries from Govern­ment.

Then, again, Government are, we understand, making inquires into the
special circumstances of the Kingwilliamstown district. We are only
sorry the inquiry seems to stop short of having the grounds of the
grievances of the Natives from themselves. It is, moreover, matter for
regret this course should have been somewhat delayed, for it would have
spared us the painful duty of denouncing those whom we had learned to
consider the friends of the Natives as worse than enemies. We are wont
to regard Sir GORDON SPRIGG as the enemy of our people but he ever was
ready to consider what repre­sentations they had to make. But even this
small modicum of con­sideration seemed to be denied the Natives by the
present Government ' in this matter; and they were thus reluctantly
compelled to put them down as even worse than their enemies. Well, it is
not for , us to anticipate the result of the investigations now being
conducted. They are very useful to our people as showing that the
Ministry in ' office are not callous or careless as to the sufferings of
the Natives a« our people were beginning to tear they were. It will be
an evil day if Natives should think so of any Government. We shall wait
to see what the upshot of the present

Bond nominee, Mr. MOOLMAN ; and we do hope that our leaders in the
Border Circle will exhibit similar firmness and resolution aa will show
the Bond that they cannot have their way in a constituency over­whelmed
by Independents and their allies the Natives. In the Midland Circle that
eminently practical body, the Midland Political As­sociation, which has
a soul larger than its body, has convened a meeting for Monday evening
next at Graaff-reinet to decide upon the gentleman who will be put
forward in the name of the Independents. This is as it should be; and we
trust the next Upper House may be graced by a strong phalanx of men of
education and independence capable of discharging the very responsible
functions of reviewing and revising the measures passed in the Assembly.

THE draft constitution that finds favour with the Responsible Government
party in Natal makes generous provision for the elevation of the
Natives. The ap­pointment of a permanent Under Secretary

is recommended, and £20,000 per annum are put down as the minimum amount
guaranteed for the elevation of. the Natives. We believe similar
provision has been made in the Cape Colony ; but unless the Ministry of
fortunate in their choice of an under Secretary for Native Affairs, and
come to an understanding with the people themselves as to how the money
is to be spent, we know from experience that the benevolence of a
well-meaning party might, as in the Cape, be rendered nugatory—or worse.

AFTER a futile attempt by the Bond to dragoon the S.E. Circle to accept
their nominee Mr. H. J. Moolman as one of the representatives in the
Legislative Council, that gentleman is now reported to have gracefully
retired on finding that Port Elizabeth, Uitenhage and Grahams- town
resented strongly Bond dictition. and that these centres had determined
to put forward three and only three gentle­men—Messrs. Wilmot, Brister
and Dolley—for the available seats. This is as it ought to be; for the
Bond have the mono­poly of the whole representation in the Midland and
Western Districts, and should be satisfied. It will be a misfor­tune if
bad generalship should in the Border Circle give the Bo- nd men a show
where by far the largest majority of the electors are not of that
school, and here too we hope electors will have the good sense to fix
upon three candidates who should courageously work their way in, in
spite of the Bond.

“ IMVO continues its vigorous protest against the re-appointment of
Native Inspectors for Kingwilliamstown, and its claim to rank the King
natives as before all other natives. It does not attempt to deal with
the chief argument in favour of the step, namely, that either the system
is bad and should be abolished all round, or that it is good and should
be impartially applied. Why make excep­tion of the King natives ? ” So
says the E. P. Herald. While our contemporary has done us the honour to
notice our articles, it would appear that it has not taken the trouble
to read before commenting on them. We have not attempted in these
columns to cloak or dissemble our convictions as to the futility of the
system. Government itself saw it when it cancelled the appoint­ments in
1882. Surely these facts should dispose even of the “chief argument” of
our “friend the enemy.” For our­selves we verily believe nothing would
be lost while good would accrue if the system were “abolished all
round.” It was merely the product of distrust be­gotten of suspicion of
our countrymen ; nothing more nor less, and it is behind, the times.

the field fighting the Bond in every legitimate way; and the
Graaffreinet Advertiser has most courageously stood alone in those parts
(while all the other journals had bent the knee to the Bond image)
exposing and de­nouncing the mode of working of that association. We
think the general revulsion of feeling now noticeable throughout the
Midlands, against the Bond may fairly be said to be the sole achievement

contemporary on such signal suc­cess. As a result Sir II STOCKEN- STROM
a candidate for the N.E. Circle will not only come in where, in the

STOCKENSTROM rightly observed the whole Native Question lay in a
nutshell.

It could be reduced to the simple one of justice as between man and man,
