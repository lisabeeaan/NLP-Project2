THERE is much, we think, to commend the view of some that the
suppression by Government of the sale of hop-beer a non-alcoholic drink
is in the interest of Cape Brandy and drunkenness; but we are unwilling
to believe that the present Cabinet, however determined they may be to
out-Sprigg Sprigg in serving the Bond, would deliberately resort to such
extremes. But while the country has been crying out for measures to deal
eventually with the Brandy evil, it is scarcely the thing to do to
proceed hastily to place frivolous obstacles to the sale of temperance
beverages for which no public demand had been made.

WITHOUT doubt the chief event of the past week has been the opening of
the Railway to Bloemfontein and the festivi­ties connected therewith.
The various South African States were represented— and highly
represented too—at the opening: each having sent its leading man or men.
The Cape had there its Governor, the Premier, the Colonial Secretary,
and the Commissioner; Natal was represented by her Governor, the
Transvaal having sent Dr. Leyds and Dr. Krause the State Attorney. An
opportunity was made for the various representations to speak ; and the
burden of their speeches was, as might have been expected in these
circumstances, Union The purely festive part of the proceed­ings came
off without a hitch. It is hardly necessary to say the arrangements were
simply grand.

WE are well aware that it is not usual to go behind the curtain in the
produc­tion of a newspaper to take account of those who see the type and
discharge generally the manual offices which are so essential to
publication ; but we are tempted to make an exception in the case of the
Burghersdorp Gazette and Bond Organ. As the name implies, it is a paper
that has no faith in anything a Native may do. But most people will be
surprised to learn that in the Bond Organ office the staff of
compositors con­sists wholly and absolutely of sable Africans—not
Afrikanders — from the foreman down. It is not from any desire to sound
a note of contention that will jar with the present festive season ; for
if anything the announcement of this fact is quite in accord with the
message to the Season—“ Good will toward men.”

Minister at Carnarvon. Should Dr. Kotze accept a requisition there is a
strong probability that he will come in, as he is an old resident of
Richmond (in the Province) widely known and respect­ed in those parts.
The Dutch church in­fluence which is very strong will be cast in the
scale of the Independents who are alone a power to be reckoned with.
Great benefit will accrue to the country when men of Dr. Kotze’s stamp
find their way into Parliament; and we wish he would be returned.

It is a notorious fact that, most, if not all. the troubles in which Cape Colonists have been involved with Natives in the past, almost invariably culminated in war about this time of the year. Thus then in this country the natural thing to do lor observant guardians of the public interest is to survey the situation to note the conditions in so far as they do or do not relate to public tranquility. This Christmas the observer is met at the very threshold of such an inquiry by the accounts of internecine strife in Pondoland. Although we are not prepared to credit the accounts, given in a dithyrambric vein by the Natal papers, of the disturbances just now prevailing in Pondoland, there can be no question as to the fact that the leading men of that territory have embarked on a u Kilkenny Cats ” policy which may end disastrously for its independence. We confess we should not relish the idea of Pondoland coming under the Cape Government so long as there exists forces within the Colony which strongly make for Glengreying ; ” and for this reason we have never ceased to urge the Pondos to approach the High Commissioner  with a proposal to be taken over by the Imperial Government on the same conditions that the Basutos are under Her Majesty’s Government, if only to secure them their lands from the capacious maw of the land-grabber. We consider they are foolish to proceed to devour each other instead of working to this end. In regard to Basutoland it is satisfactory to note from discussions at the recent Pitso that the people regard with distrust the efforts of the chiefs to grasp power  They are for reverting to the old order of things when the Queen and Moshesh were the recognised  chiefs. Within the Colony we have written enough to show that the brusque unsympathetic attitude of the Native Affairs Department towards the Natives, and its plotting in matters such as the Glen Grey Native lands, have served seriously to undermine the confidence of these people not only in a Department that has ever set itself against J the Native interest, but in the Cabinet which is identified with such action. In these circumstances it would be the sheerest hypocrisy for us to bandy about the usual compliments of the Season since they have no meaning for our people.

Sir,—The Farmers’ Association at Kei Road, on the motion of Mr. John Landrey, jun., insists on the Government putting in force the “Location Act” which has for some time been in abeyance. We don’t hear of any reason being given for this step. The Natives are peaceable and quiet, and, generally speaking, law- abiding and prosperous; hence the. conclusion some people have come to for this unseemly haste is that somebody wants a billet; but it would be uncharitable to suppose that anyone of the name “ Landrey” is included in that category.

The utter uselessness of Location Inspectors may be seen in the Komgha Division, where an Inspector gets about £300 per year for duties which can be performed ten times more efficiently by the Cape Police. Where you have a splendid Police force, like that of Commissioner Davies’, Location Inspectors are nothing more or less than costly obstructions. I hope the Government will not stultify itself by listening to the howling of a few chronic place hunters.
