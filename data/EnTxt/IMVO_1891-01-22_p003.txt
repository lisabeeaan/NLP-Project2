  R. PEACOCK’S candidature
  for the Eastern Province

finds cordial support in the Kaffrarian Watchman. But the views of this
gentleman on the Native Question—and we are glad this is the only matter
on which difference of view is professed—are distrusted by our
contemporary. Mr. PEACOCK, in the Watchman’s estimation, holds wrong
views because he has ventured to throw out the opinion
in his reply to one of the requisitions addressed to him. that—

The Native question is always important,
and all matters affecting the welfare of this
important portion of our population, within
and without the Colony, will receive my
earnest attention.

Education suited to them, and that will
fit them for useful work, and increase their
value as the main source of the labour
supply of this country, should be liberally
assisted and directed.

I would also support measures to save
them from demoralization by the unrestricted sale of strong drink, and
certainheathen customs, and also to give them security in their land
tenure. . . . The unrestricted manufacture of a spirit which
is both cheap and bad is a great evil in any
country, hut in this Colony will be disastrous
to the Native races, and thus destroy the
labour supply, which is so valuable.

To such comprehensive but innocent views the Watchman takes exception.
For it writes ;—

It is when we come to what we may term “sentimental ” politics that we
feel that we and a large body of the electors are not in full accord
with Mr. Peacock. With us the conviction hardens that a hybrid scheme of
what is called Native Policy is impracticable and unwise. Wo must either
go upon the plan of a paternal government treating the Natives as a
people in a state of adolescence not prepared to undertake the
responsibilities nor to exercise the privileges of men in a more
advanced stage of development, or else we must go upon democratic lines
and make no difference between them and other qualified subjects of the
State. We ere shut up however to a democratic constitution, and whilst
we give the Natives the privileges conferred thereby, we must
require them to submit to its responsibilities and they must shape their
habits and seek for the enjoyment of their own peculiar customs
according as the circumstances of their new political environment
persuit. Probably the day of the extremists in “ sentimental” politics
in this colony is past and hence we may with more freedom than we should
otherwise feel disposed to
exercise give Mr. Peacock’s candidature support, hoping that when be
comes to exercise the functions of a legislator he will banish sentiment
and stick close to the lines of a constitution, which knows of no
distinction between subjects on race and colour lines.

Yes; but when, a few weeks back, a- proposal was mooted to subject the
Natives to special vicious measures under the high sounding phrases of
Location Inspectorship, Location Supervision, we heard nothing then
about Natives being treated in the same
manner as their white neighbours. Again, it is only a few years ago that
this very journal strongly advocated the disfranchisement of Natives
whose qualification was derived from buildings and land. If our
contemporary had its way the principle of the identical treatment of
white and black would only be carried out in such things

as the free trade in bad brandy for the Natives, and the dispossession
of what lands they still have. When, however, such treatment is to be
applied in preserving the Natives’ privileges and saving their lives it
is objected to and denounced and discredited. Clearly this is a case of
“ Heads I win, tails you lose ” that will deceive no one.

THE Government are to be congratulated on the wise and satisfactory
adjustment of the subject of the Locations supervision in these
districts. It is a source of great gratification to us to be in a
position definitely to acquaint our readers with the fact that the
Location Inspectors’ matter has been finally and fully settled ; and in
a manner that is acceptable to all
concerned. It will bo recollected that the Estimates framed by the
outgoing Government provided for no fewer than seven new Location
Inspectors—one for Stutterheim, four for Kingwilliamstown, and two for
East London. The Estimates
were adopted by the incoming Ministry although, as we all know, they
were constitutionally not bound by what had been done by their
predecessors. These appointments, it will be remembered, were almost
made at one time ; but we
arc satisfied this could not have been
done except under a misapprehension and on erroneous information
furnished to the head of the department. This the permanent Under
Secretary for Native Affairs must be held responsible for; and we have
long known him as an officer
whose opinions on questions of vitalinterest to the Natives are the
reverse of orthodox. If ever the Natives were on their good behaviour
and were devoted to legitimate peaceful avocations, it was when the
changes were mooted to reform the Native affairs administration in a
manner that would have resulted prejudicially to the peach
and happiness of the Natives. Well, we all know what happened. No
efforts were spared on behalf of the Native people to bring the real
facts of the question before the Government and before tho country. In
the end Mr. PERCY NIGHTINGALE than
whom no fairer-minded gentleman could be desired, and one who has,by
sheer uncompromising devotion to the interests of truth and right,
deservedly won the confidence of successive Ministries—was asked to look
into the matter and report. No sooner has the real state of the case
been made known to the
Government, and a plan devised for making matters comfortable for the
parties concerned, than it has been adopted. It is most reassuring to
feel that there is no desire on the part of Mr. FAURE or any member of
the Government to be unjust or unkind towards the Natives, or to worry
them in any way unnecessarily. There is, as is
well-known, a party with representatives iu our very midst, whose views
and intentions are the very anti thesis of these ; and this party, being
strong and having much political influence, has to be reckoned with to
some extent. So, to meet their wishes, there are to be two instead of
seven Inspectors. One of these will be for the Stutterheim district, to
supervise farm Locations, and the other will be for the private
Locations on farms in the Kingwilliamstown division. Mr.
DICK, with Mr. CROZIER as helper, will take charge of the Tribal or
Government Locations, which he has always superintended ; and Messrs.
VERITY and KING will exercise the same functions over the Locations they
have supervised satisfactorily, on the whole, for the last
seven years. These gentlemen, with ths assistance of the Headmen, will
frame Registers of stock once a quarter. This is by no means a new
provision; as, for statistical purposes in the past, Natives have had
their stock counted by members of the police force. The branding section
will be held in abeyance, but people will be invited and encouraged to
adopt a mark or brand of their own and apply it voluntarily. The
branding will not be enforced even on the private Locations, but means
for the identification of stock will be encouraged. At East London it is
not deemed necessary to disturb the existing order, for, owing to the
oversight of the efficient and admirable force—
the Cape Police—the result of the large-hearted plans of Sir THOMAS
SCANLEN, stock-thefts may be numbered among the things that were. The
scheme saves the country nearly £1,500 a year on the estimated
expenditure; and will cause no friction in its

for many years past. Mr. TAPSON, ex-inspector of Locations, is
ap­pointed over the farm Locations uf Kingwilliamstown, and Mr. G. E.
NIGHTINGALE, the Inspector of Alexandria, goes to Stutterheim, his place
being taken by Mr. LLOYD, ex-inspector of East London. Such is the happy
termination of a diffi­culty which, at one time, threatened to blast the
confidence of the Natives in the friendly intentions and actions of the
new Government, and we sincerely hope no occasion for misunderstandings,
even of a temporary nature, may arise in the future.

IN mentioning, for the THE EASTERN first time we believe, Mr. CONTEST.
J. L. Bradfield’s candi­dature for the Eastern Circle, the Frontier
Guardian, which is published in the district where Mr. Bradfield
resides, announces the fact of an influential deputation that waited bn
Mr. Mark Moss of that town asking him to offer himself as a candidate,
evi­dently in opposition to Mr. Bradfield. Fortunately Mr. Moss was not
in a posi­tion to accede to the wishes of the deputation. It was quite
to be expected that in a small community like Dordrecht, where everybody
takes an almost person­al interest in the affairs of everybody else,
strong animosities should be che­rished. These might be pardoned if
vented in connection with a district election, but when the election
affects so large an area as the whole of the Eastern Electoral Province,
we should have thought the Dordrecht cliques would have disappeared in
the purview of so wide an expanse. For ourselves we are not in a
position to appreciate the merits of the sectional disputes of the
Dordrecht people so, while we believe in Mr. Brad­field as a gentleman,
fairly and favouably known beyond the boundaries of the District in
which he resides, who would represent well the suffrages of the electors
in the extended Districts com­prised in the Province, Mr. Moss, wo
think, labours under the disadvantage of being known in Dordrecht and
that neighbourhood only.

AT the Johannesburg FLOGGING Police court on Thursday NATIVE last,
several Natives were DESERTERS, ordered to bo flogged for deserting from
the Central Mino. It is very material to know what constitutes desertion
; but certainly the punishment for the crime is degrading. 1 f it should
transpire that the natives at Johannesburg are to be subjected to such
brutal measures whenever they avail themselves of the liberty of every
freeman not to work where he does not like, that place will bear a bad
name among the labouring natives ; and inas­much as such labour is its
mainstay. Johannesburg cannot allord to make it­self a by-word to Native
labourers. WE trust this is not the first development of Mr. Kruger’s
policy to secure labourers, for then some would say it was nothing more
nor less than slavery.

IN the cloudless sky of OPPOSING the candidature of Mr. PEACOCK, has
arisen an unfore­seen sign of opposition. It has been intimated by one
of the King­williamstown newspapers that two gen­tlemen—Messrs. Baker
and Malcomess— of this town have announced an intention to thwart Mr.
Peacock’s return the reason being a belief that he is re-entering
poli­tical life to subserve private ends. This attitude has astonished
the community; for Mr. Peacock has the reputation, even among people who
do not think the same way, of being a truly honourable gentle­man, in
sending whom to Parliament any constituency would, while honour­ing him,
be honouring itself. We don’t think the opposition hinted at will have
the slightest effect on bis prospects of being returned, seeing that
opinion is so overwhelmingly in Mr. Peacock’s favour.
