still bearing bad fruit although it was done months ago. We observe that
in the Sesuto paper Lizeli- nyane, a correspondent urges all his people
not to go to Johannesburg to be lashed, but that they should breathe the

that they should keep to their homes and not go out to be a nuisance to
the white man. This is a coustruction of the high-handed proceedings at
Johannesburg for which the employers of labour were hardly prepared, we
fancy.

demur; and we sincerely hope that in Mount Frere Mr. Scully will, for
many reasons, be restrained from dealing with Makaula in the manner he
has been proceeding to­wards Jaji and Tyefu in Peddie.

MATTERS IN GLEN GREY.

[LETTER TO THE EDITOR.]

DEAR SIR,—In answer to “ Native Patriot,” I can only say that his letter
confirms my statement, for after saying that “ it was to be regretted
that I should make such a statement,” he further on says “ I quite agree
with Mr. Garrett,” etc. I am ready to admit that there are many
progressive Natives in this district especially in such places as the
Bengu and Macubeni, but I still say that as a district Glen Grey is not
progressive. I sincerely trust that our next Show will prove that I have
exaggerated. I have only stated my experience of the past, I yield to no
man in my desire to help and encourage the Native, and can call myself
their frinml, and I am a good friend if I stalo frankly what I believe
to be the truth, though it may not be palatable. If “Patript” will come
forward and work in the interests of progression then he will have no
more empty title, there are many patriots who get no further than talk,
I hope my friend is not such an one. I think it is a mistake to make
invidious comparisons between Magistrates, both gentlemen in question
are good men, and each has worked under (what I still as­sert) different
departments and methods, and everybody knows that Mr. Jenner had one of
the most difficult districts in the country, a district divided fiscally
and by Native, one part in such close proximity to Queenstown, as to
throw in their lot with that town rather than with their own district,
when we fought with our few weak voices fora Fiscal division, the Glen
Grey portion threw all their influence against us, and then accused us
of self-seeking after a paltry Road Inspec­tor and Secretaryship, such
was the action of the Patriots.

Referring to the Show Committee I was the originator of the same and can
speak with authority. We elected a large Pro­visional Committee
consisting ofEnglish , Dutch and Native. Notices was sent to every
individual “ That a meeting would be held to elect a working committee.”
The meeting took place and not a Native attended, consequently the
committee were selected from those present, and those were the names
which appeared on the Prize list. We are in thorough sympa­thy with the
Native, we are anxious to see them raised and protected, and we are
willing to work to that end, but if the Natives themselves do not assist
then it will be labour in vain.

  Thanking you for your valuable space, I am, dear sir,

Yours truly,
