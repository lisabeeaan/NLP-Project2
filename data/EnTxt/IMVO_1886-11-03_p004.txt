AT the Stockenstrom section of
electoral division of Fort Beaufort

Bond had lodged objections against a large
number of the coloured population to
Become voters. The Revising Magistrate
sat at Seymour on the 19th ultimo to
adjudicate upon the objections, the result,
as stated by a correspondent. of the
Journal, will be interesting and inspirit-
ing to our readers. The writer says:
'‘ The electoral battle, as it was called,
came off on Tuesday 19th inst. The
coloured electors turned out very strong,
and the village of Seymour was quite
astir on this dav, and subsequent days.
The President of the Bond did not put in
an appearance on Tuesday, but left Mr.
S. M. Botha and one or two more to
appear and raise objections. The first
day’s proceedings were a miserable
failure. Some who, Mr. Botha thought,
would be able to back him up in his
objections fled, or rode off home, leaving
him alone to get out of the difficulty the
best way he could. The objectors at last
became ashamed of the cause they had
taken in hand, and were simply ridiculed
for their inability. On Thursday 21st the
Bond President, Mr. N. Barnard appeared
on the scene, but not to object, but to
inform the authorities he had been led
astray by others, and wished to withdraw
his objections. So the voters lists remain
as they were, except with a good many
more additions. No erasures, only those
who are dead or have left the district. The
coloured electors seem proud of their
victory, and feel certain they are able to
hold their own against the Bond mem-
bers. During the whole time our respected
Magistrate displayed that impartiality for
which he is so admired; always pains-
taking in trying to dispense justice to
everyone.”

THE Magistrate of Fort Peddie sits on Tuesday next (10th November) to
dispose of objections urged by the Peddie Euro­peans against our people.
The natives have, we think, taken the best security it is possible for
them to take for the fair and satisfactory trial of their cause by
enga­ging Mr. Rose-Innes, one of the best Solicitors in town. They may
well take courage from the Seymour proceedings, and note the importance
of attending to combat the random statements of their enemies.
