Disfranchisement Assembly Division

^(Division) on the subject of Native Disfranchisement is remarkable. It
is an open secret that the Bill is in­tended for seven Frontier
constitu­encies that are mostly peopled by

Natives—constituencies that are at present represented by members who
are no admirers of the policy of the Bond, that august institution
manipulated from the Western district which slowly works for the uniting
of the Cape Colony with the Dutch Republics on terms acceptable to the
Transvaal, and republicans in general. It is felt that the union is at
present impossible because the Cape Colony will continue to conduct its
government on the fundamental principles of British freedom and
political equality of all under its flag—a policy which is heartily
abominated by the Republics. The Cape Colony must needs, therefore,
level its policy up or down to that of the Republics. As a preliminary
step to this end the British rights of the Natives must be abridged; the
British element in the constituencies must be weakened, so that their
opponents should have a walk over. The necessary majority secured at the
forthcoming general election, and the troublesome independent members
purged by what has correctly been described as SPRIGG’S Purge,— unequal
laws will follow, and the Genius of Boer Republicanism is appeased.

It is in this wise that the solid phalanx of thirty-five Bondmen and
Bond dependents in the legislature may be accounted for in the division.
The three Ministers who assisted them are at present birds of a feather.
The other six members who complete the forty-four in favour of Native
Disfranchisement, are members of British descent, who are personally
devoted to members of the Ministry, although their training and
instincts are British. The measure is, we are glad to say, opposed by
twenty-three members who constitute the very quintessence of the
intelligence of the House. It will be with much satisfaction that our
people will find that some of the gentlemen they know who, however, have
always been associated with a section of the House that is regarded as
callous and careless as to native interests, were on the side that was
against the stifling of the natives. The natives expect no ex- ceptional
favours from the Parliament and the Government of the country, they look
for nothing more than what they are entitled to under the law of the
land ; and the earnest speeches of Captain BRABANT, Mr. FROST, Mr.
PEARSON, and Mr. WALKER, gentlemen who preferred to sever themselves
from their former leaders in favour of right, will not soon be forgotten
by the native people in this country. The way, too, in which Mr. CROSBIE
and Mr. WIENER voted, for a similar reason, will ever be re- membered.
Natives are not so depraved as to be unable to distinguish between those
who are their friends and those who are their enemies in the House. And
we confess to having been very much disappointed by Mr. WILLIAM AYLIFF,
Mr. JOHN WOOD, Mr. HOCKLY, Mr. GOLDSCHMIDT, and Mr. BERGH—gentlemen whom
we had learnt to regard as not unfriendly to British freedom and native
rights - had on this occasion assisted to swell the ranks of those who
are against the privileges of the native people. We firmly believe that,
in a short time, it will be found out that, by this measure the party
that favours British institutions because they are based on justice—the
party of intelligent and independent men—has been weakened in the House
beyond measure by this Bill of Disfranchisement, and the House will
simply be packed by a strong blind ; majority of Bondmen, prejudiced not
only against the natives, but against the English element in this
Colony. It must not, therefore, be supposed that the natives are the
only losing party by this enactment. At present the invincible Bond
phalanx can do nothing unless helped by eight or nine non-Bond members.
The passing of the Bill will make it independent of these even.

  Revised Constitution of the Mie
  Educational Association.

  The following is the proposed constitution of this body which will be
  discussed at its meeting at Peelton next week:—

  OBJECT OF THE ASSOCIATION.

  I. The object of this Association is Defined to be to take a special
  interest in educational matters, and to promote the agricultural,
  commercial, and the general interests of the natives.

  FORMATION OF THE ASSOCIATION.

II. The Association shall be composed of local Branches (consisting of
      not less than ten members each), to be formed upon the authority
      obtained from the parent Association, which was established in
      1880, in the various districts of the
      colony.

  QUALIFICATION OF MEMBERSHIP.

III. The membership of this Association shall consist of residents of
       the districts of the Colony who shall have paid to its lunds such
       quarterly subscriptions as may be defined by the particular
       Branch with which they are connected and have
       signified their adherence to the Constitution of the Association.

BRANCH EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE.

IV. Each Branch Association shall be presided over by an Executive
      Committee to consist of not less than five members, who shall
      choose their own chairman ana officers. The committee to be
      elected for one year at each annual meeting of the
      Branch Association, or otherwise as often as vacancies may occur.

  BRANCH ANNUAL MEETINGS.

V.  The Annual Meetings of the Branch Association shall be held not
      later than September in each year, at such places as each Branch
      may decide upon.

DUTIES OF EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE.

VI. The Duties of the Executive Committee shall be to further the
      objects of the association as above defined, having regard also to
      the interests of their own districts.

VII. Each Branch may frame its own bye-laws for the wording of the
       Branch, which shall be laid for approval before the next ensuing
       meeting of the Council herein-after described, and which shall
       not be repugnant to the Constitution of the Association.

  GENERAL CONGRESS.

VIII. A Congress of the Association shall be held annually in January,
        (at such place as shall be fixed at the Congress of the previous
        year) to consist of members elected at the annual branch
        meetings of the various branches. Each branch consisting of less
        than 50 members shall have the right to elect one
        representative, and branches consisting of over fifty members
        two representatives to the congress. For every fifty members
        above 100 each branch has the power to send one more
        representative to the Congress.

  N.B.—The formation of branch Associations will be notified to the
  Executive Committee of the parent Association for the time being, and
  it shall be the duty of that Committee to make arrangements for the
  General Congress.

COUNCIL OF THE ASSOCIATION.

IX. The Congress at its annual meeting shall elect a Council consisting
      of President, Vice-President, General Secretary, General Treasurer
      and four members, who shall conduct the general business of the
      Association, and shall hold office till the next annual meeting of
      the Congress.

  N.B.—The Council shall meet as often as may be necessary to transact
  the business of the Association.

  CONTRIBUTIONS TO THE GENERAL FUND.

X.  Each Branch Association shall pay to the Funds of the General
      Association the sum of — for each member of the said branch, to be
      paid in Quarterly instalments to the General Treasurer.

  J. TENGO-JABAVU,
  Convener of Committee.
