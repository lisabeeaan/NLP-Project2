Others, who ostentatiously boast
that they are “ moderates,” while i

rejecting Mr. HOFMEYR’S scheme of plural votes, advocate the raising of
the franchise pure and simple with the same end in view, namely, to
counteract the Native vote. The “moderates” also proceed on the false
assumption that Natives are all on the Registration Rolls on the £25
limit. We know that such is not the case; and as a matter of fact only a
very limited number of our people, and that the pick among them, find a
place on the Re­gister on the present qualifica­tion. Those who attend
the Registration Courts of the country would acknowledge the extreme
moderation of this statement. It will be said that a people whose landed
property can scarcely come up to £25 have no stake in the country, and
should not have a vote. But the fact of the matter, in so far as our
people are concerned, is, that while they compare favourably with other
classes in wealth, that wealth most unfortunately does not show itself
in the possession of a decent dwelling house and land. If the vote
depended on income, and on movable as well as immovable property, it
would be found that these people were not so badly off as some seemed to
think. With the Natives the acquisition of land and a decent house
involves a fun­damental change in the mode of living, and has nothing to
do with poverty. We trust that the “ moderates ” will see that the £25
franchise is not too low ; since it has all these forty years served to
allow of the more pushing and the more intelligent among the people to
take a share—and a small share it is —in the affairs of the country.
That this class will continue to increase is our fervent hope. At
present it is so small that it passes our com­prehension how the country
has become concerned about it. Be­sides when it has succeeded to free
itself from the shackles of barbar­ism to acquire fixed property to the
amount of £25 it is simply a question of time for it to raise it to £75
or £100. So, unless, then, those who are for raising the Fran­chise are
prepared to say that the right to vote must be kept for all time above
the beads of the blacks, simply because of their colour, we fail to see
what it is they are to secure by the simple raising of the
qualification. Thus we come to the conclusion that it is neither by the
plural vote for whites nor by the mere raising of the Franchise
qualification that the dreaded spectre of Native influence in the
affairs of the Colony can be neutralised. A judicious Re­distribution of
Seats scheme is all that appears to be capable of being defended on any
grounds of sound policy; and as the matter opens up a subject by itself
brimful of suggestions, it must be dealt with separately.

BRUTAL FRANKNESS.

THE pious people who have been

saying that the Bond had no aims of seizing political power in raising
the franchise business, will be disabused Dy what Ous Land, the organ of
moderate Afrikanderism, has to say on the subject. Considering the
subject by the light of American experience, Ons Land remarks :—

It may be contended that in America the circumstances are different from
what they are here, because there the white population is opposed to the
negro vote, and all the whites have the same end in view. Here at the
Cape, the whites are divided into two large classes, and we find that
the English are, generally speaking, in favour of allowing the Kafirs
the right of voting, whilst the Dutch al­most unanimously desire at
least to see that right restricted. It cannot be denied that the
difference of opinion is chiefly due to the fact that the one party is
in favour of the native vote for the sake of the political capital that
can be made out of it. For this very reason, however, we think an
educational test all the more desirable in the Cape Colony. If the
opinions of the two main classes of the community are often at variance
and their interests seems to be antagonistic to each other, no good will
be done and no improvement in the situation be effected, if one party is
allowed to utilise against the other the votes of natives, who neither
possess any property worth mentioning, nor are educated sufficiently to
exercise their own intelligence as voters.

Here we have the matter stated by a Dutch newspaper as one of Dutch
versus English; and Mr. SIVEWRIGHT said at Steynsburg, a few months
back, that if it were so the Government would not even consider the
subject. Of course the Commissioner's asserveration depends on what he
should deem the best evidence to establish the case. But when both sides
agree that the question to be decided is one governed by the
considerations of race aggrandisement, it is high time a government that
wished to promote concord among the different peoples comprising the
population of the country let well alone Ons Land, it need scarcely be
stated, is entirely mistaken in the opinion that the votes of Natives “
are utilised ” by the English against

the Dutch; the fact being that the Natives would spurn any attempt of
the kind. Nor can our Dutch contemporary cite a single instance wherein
our people ever voted for Englishmen with views known to be anti-Native,
simply because of their nationality; and unless it can make its word
good in this respect Ons Land has no business to make the base charge
that our people “ are utilised ” by any race or party. On the contrary
we know of cases where Natives have actually voted for Dutchmen and
rejected Englishmen, because of the views represented by the contending
candidates. Let Ons Land take care how it writes in this matter or it
will find itself in a fearful mess.
