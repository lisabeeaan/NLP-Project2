be without interest to speculate on
the election chances with respect to
the new constituencies of Tembu-
land and East Griqualand. For
the two seats there are candidates
representing the views of each
school of thought in our Parlia-
ment. We have Colonel GRIFFITH
for Tembuland, and Mr. MORRIS,
for East Griqualand, who favour a
policy of progress generally, while
dealing out even-handed justice to
white and black alike. Then there
are Mr. SIVEWRIGHT, for East
Griqualand, and Mr. CLOETE, for
Tembuland, professing to be im-
bued with progressive views, al-
though allied with a retrograde

party in Parliament—a party, the
cardinal points of whose policy is
that, Africanders are the only
people in this Colony, all others,
who are . contemptuously termed
rooineks and schepsels being aliens,
to serve whom the Government
should not strain itself. Such are
the clear dividing lines between the
rival candidates for the Transkeian
seats. Mr. ZEITSMAN, also for East
Griqualand, cannot be classified, as
he places himself before the electors
as one who is everything to all men.

With his particular opinions, Mr.
CLOETE finds favour with the Xala-
nga Bondmen, on whom at one
time it was believed he would have
solely to depend for his support.
That vote totals quite five hundred
as against its estimated strength of
a thousand, when it was thought it
would dictate its own terms to the
other sections of the electorate.
There are, however, in the con-
stituency a number of the silly and
weak who, although not fettered
by Bond gives, must needs hop
into the net spread for the thought-
less At the outside these could
not exceed one hundred. We
argue on these data that Mr.
CLOETE’S poll would be about six
hundred. Against the five hundred
Bondmen at Xalanga, there are
two hundred Natives in the same
division, who are sure to favour
Colonel GRIFFITH’S opinions. The
purely English opportunities of
Butterworth and Umtata are sure
to muster in favour of the candidate
with fair views towards all, about
four hundred votes. Thus bringing
Colonel GRIFFITH to Mr. CLOETE’S
six hundred, and the stray votes of
Natives in Fingoland and Tembu-
land Proper, as also those of traders,
except the nine who form the
Tembuland Association, must assure
Colonel GRIFFITH’S success, and
seal the defeat of Mr. CLOETE.

It is not feo easy to forecast the
issues of the East Griqualand con-
test. If noise and bravado are
indispensable to win a constituency,
then, Mr. SIVEWRIGHT should win
hands down; but the cackling
of his supporters in the Kokstad
Supplement would seem to in-
dicate that they are conscious
of the paucity of their number.
It must not be forgotten that Mr.
DONALD STRACHAN is said to com-
mand quite fourteen hundred native
voters in the Umzimkulu district
fur Mr. SIVEWRIGHT, and this
number holds the field against all
the other districts put together.
If the matter were properly placed
before our people we are not so
sure whether a good many of these
people might no desert Mr.
STRACHAN. Were not this Umzi-
mkulu vote so overpowering, the
chances are that Mr. MORRIS would
be the elect of the division It
strikes us that there is not much
to choose between the supporters of
Mr. SIVEWRIGHT and those of Mr.
ZEITSMAN. Their views are as un-
stable as water; and the result of ask-
ing Mr. ZEITSMAN to retire from the
field would have been to gratuitously
strengthen Mr. SIVEWRIGHT. For
our part we think that Mr. MORRIS’S
chance depends upon Mr. SIVE-
WRIGHT and Mr. ZEITSMAN going
to the poll. They divide the
electors of the invertebrate type,
while Mr. MORRIS brings up his
men with definite and uncompro-
mising principles. Because of the
Umzimkulu vote Mr. SIVEWRIGHT’S
prospects are good, and we should
