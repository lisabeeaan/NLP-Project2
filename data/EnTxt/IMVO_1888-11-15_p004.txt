THE elections which came oft’ last week are those in connection with the
representation of Aliwal North and of Kingwilliamstown. At both these
places the candidates before the electors were four in favour of the
Sprigg Ministry, and four against. Mr. Sauer and Mr. Everitt Orsmond ran
as Oppositionists at Aliwal North, and Mr. Solomon and Mr. Warren at
King; while Mr. Bekker and Mr. De Wet were put forward in the Sprigg (or
Bond) interest at the former place, and Mr. Schermbrucker and Dr. Egan
represented the same principles in Kingwilliamstown. The result is that
three stern Opposition men have been returned, and only one
Ministerialist—Mr. Schermbrucker—has been successful. The election at
these two places makes no difference in the strength of parties, as the
loss of Mr. Solomon in King is made up by the gain in the return of Mr.
Orsmond at Aliwal.

THE elections, so far as they have gone, show that, of the thirtv
members elected, the Sprigg party can boast of eight devoted supporters:
while the Opposition number eleven members, and the remaining ten
acknowledge Mr. Hofmeyr as their leader. Sir D. Tennant is, of course,
neutral. There is, therefore, a strong probability that in the next
Parliament parties will be evenly divided.

ALTHOUGH Dr. Stewart is for the present from home, a representative of
this journal was pleased to find last week that the work at Lovedale was
going on as satisfactorily as one could wish. The appearance of the
place seems to be im­proving every year, notwithstanding the fact that
in the anxiety of the present Ministry to stand well with the farmers
the education grants towards this most useful Institution have been cut
down frightfully. The number of pupils is larger than it has been for
the last three or four years, and with the reduced grants the managers
of the Institution are at their wits’ ends to make both ends meet.
During the writer’s visit the denizens o Lovedale were in a somewhat
excited state of mind over the elections, as on their failure to induce
some of the Institution voters to accept their candidates, the Agents of
the candidates at Alice revenged them­selves by thrusting on the
Institution honours it would not accept—namely that it was setting
itself up as an electioneer­ing machine. For our part we don’t see why a
place possessing the large interests in Victoria East which Lovedale
has, should be without a representative in Parliament; and it would not
be a bad idea if in the Redistribution of Seats of Bill, provision were
made for the presence in Parliament of an honourable member for
Lovedale. As past residents, we observed that the Board are not
unmind­ful of introducing new blood on the staff, for we found that Rev.
T. D. Philip, B.A. (London). Mr. Hallack, B.A. (Cape), Mr. H. Brinckmann
were grafted on the staff. We missed among former masters the familiar
faces of Mr. Smith, M.A. (Aberd.), and Mr. A. W. Roberts who is away on
furlough. Mr. and Mrs. Geddes appear to be doing well. We wish the
Institu­tion increased prosperity.

THE E. L. Dispatch attributes the solidity of the Native vote against
Mr. Schermbrucker, not to conviction, but to coaching and flat
misrepresentation. Those who have had anything to do with the
Kingwilliamstown contest will un­hesitatingly say that our contemporary
does not know what it is writing about.
