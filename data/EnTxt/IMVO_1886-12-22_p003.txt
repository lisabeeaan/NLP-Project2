A CORRESPONDENT writes:—On Wed­nesday evening, the 8th instant, the
members of the Wesleyan Native Church, Cala, had a social tea meeti'ng
in the Native Agricultural Hall. There was a good and pleasing
gathering. What with singing, praying, and speeches, a delight­ful
evening was spent—and we trust a profitable one. The Resident Magistrate
(Mr. C. J. Levey) having returned only a few days before from his
European trip, the opportunity was taken of giving him a hearty welcome
in the name of the Wesleyan Methodist Church here. Dun- can was deputed
on behalf of the people to address Mr. Levey, which he did. Mr. Levey
spoke to the people in a pleasing and encouraging way. “ God Save the
Queen ” was sung heartily while he was present. Several of the people
had walked a considerable distance to be present at this Christian
gathering.

IN some utterances which Sir Gordon Sprigg made at Graham's Town, he
gave the following statesmanlike reason for the policy the Ministry
pursued towards the Pondos :—“With the knowledge that they had the
strength of the whole colony at their back, and that they had also the
moral as well as the material support of the Imperial Government and of
the people of England behind them, the Colonial Government could afford
to be moderate, and they had shown their strength by acting towards the
Pondos with moderation, and they had exercised it towards a tribe which
they might have crushed had they wished to do so.” Were these sentiments
to be the key-note of the second Sprigg Ministry in all native matters,
it will not take long for the Pre­mier to rehabilitate his character in
the eyes of moderate colonists and of the natives of this country.

MR. BROWNLEE is attempting in another column to prove to us numskulls
that “crush the Pond os ” was not what he proposed ; but it is very
singular that all in the country who read his regrettable letter to the
Mercury, ran away with the idea that he proposed the crushing, and have
taken sides on it as such. He is however, the best living expositor of
his thoughts, and we are bound to accept his denial as to ever having
shared Sir Bartle Frere’s reported saying that “ the Pondos must be
crushed.”
