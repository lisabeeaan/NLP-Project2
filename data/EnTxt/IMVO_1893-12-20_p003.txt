  MOLTENO of Capetown. The name

  country. Sir JOHN MOLTENO, tho
  head of the family was, under the
  sobriquet of “Lion of Beaufort,”
  if not the foremost leader, at any
  rate in the front rank of the
  leading Parliamentarians of his
  day who nobly fought for tho in-
  troduction of self Government into
  the Cape Colony ; and, he side by
  side with Mr SAUL SOLOMON, Mr.
  PORTER, Mr. (now Chief Justice)
  DE VILLIERS, and other leading
  men of the time saw that form of
  Government, under which no mean
  progress has been made by the

country, fairly launched on its course. Indeed, Sir JOHN MOL- TENO’S
name will always occupy an exalted place in the annals of the country as
that of the first Premier who held office under Responsible Government.
It is meet, therefore, that when most political leaders of the day are
raising tho banner to battle against the prostitution of the real
prin­ciples of Responsible Government now the distinguishing feature of
our politics, a son and namesake of the great Responsible leader should
be in evidence. Mr. J. C. MOLTENO’S name figures in the contest in
Tembuland. as a protest against the flaccid and backboneless political
principles of the candidates now in the field; and he comes forward in
deference to the strong request of a considerable number of electors who
desire to record their votes for Principle in politics as opposed to the
Opportunism that seemed lately likely to pervade everything in our
politics. We wish his candidature great success, and we trust the poll
will show that there are enough men in the wide expanse comprising the
constituency of Tembuland who value sound and saving principle in public
life. The country, we are certain, has no confidence in men who chop and
change in their views because of office as has recently been done by Sir
G. SPRIGG, Messrs. LAING and FROST. On the other hand there are many who
desire to show by their votes that they believe in men who are prepared
to relinquish office with its emoluments for what they deem principle.
This is the question before the country; and on it many in Tembuland
will record their votes. Mr. J. 0. MOLTENO’S candidature will afford
these the opportunity they so fer­vently desired. That the candidate who
holds these principles will head the poll is rendered more certain by
the fact that those who do not set store by Principles in politics are
hopelessly divided against themselves.

  URING the past two weeks
  the various schools have

been breaking up for their vaca-
tions, and the movements of pupils
returning from school to their
homes mark the close of the School
term as well as of the year. News-
papers, besides, are teeming with
accounts of the closing meetings,
or “ speech days ” of most educa-
tional establishments. All these
and other signs indicate that the
work of the education of the voung
is not being neglected. It is a
work from which the country
stands to derive the greatest ad-
vantages. For tho more widely
diffused education is in a commu-
nity the better it is in every way
for tho country. This remark
applies with greater force, perhaps,
to the circumstances and conditions
of our people, the bulk of whom
are still beyond the rayon of being
benefited by education. It will
not be disputed we think that the
commercial value of the average
educated or civilised, or even semi-
civilised Native is fifty times that
of a raw man with few if any wants.
This granted, is it not the highest
duty of our Government to strain
every nerve to civilise every Native
in tho land as soon as possible for
tho good of tho commonwealth at
large ? Of course it is. It is in
view of this that we have to deplore
the trend of Dr. MUIR’S policy,
which is being voiced throughout
the frontier at the present time to
cut down or cripple weaker and
struggling schools. This is in
direct opposition to the policy of
his respected predecessor of foster-
ing feeble causes until they were
strong enough to shift for them-
selves. That Sir LANGHAM DALE’S
line of action was the more reason-
able it is readily seen, while the
logical result of Dr. MUIR’S is to
cut down schools till nothing
remains of them. People do not
pay taxes to the state to encourage
experiments such as the one which
the present. Superintendent-General
of Education seems to be bent
up HI ; and Natives are being given
good cause to complain when their
schools are being whittled down by
degrees with scarcely any warning.
Dr. MUIR’S severe policy recalls to
our mind what JOHN BRIGHT once
characterised as feeding a hungry
dog cn its own tail. To say that
the Natives sadly need education
is to state a truism, but to take
advantage of a transition state to
polish off struggling schools by
crippling their resources seems
hardly the true policy for the state
to pursue, and we trust that in the
coming time greater forbearance
will he excercised towards schools,
than would appear to have been the-
case during the past six months
or so.
