EDUCATION OFFICE.

  HE Department of Public Edu-
  cation in Capetown is pre-

sently to be manned by a staff of
entirely new officials. Sir LANG-
HAM DALE, whose name has, from
long and close association, become
synonymous with the education of
the Colony, vacates office to give
way to Dr. THOMAS MUIR, of the
Glasgow High School. The new
Superintendent General of Educa-
tion is highly spoken of as an
organiser and a scholar ; and is said
to have included the Dutch of Hol-
land in his studies. Mr. J. H.
BRADY, M.A., recently appointed
Assistant Superintendent-General
of Education, is also a new man in
the office. The appointment of an As-
sistant Superintendent-General of
Education was, however, a tempor-
ary expedient, as was explained by
Mr. SAUER in Parliament last Ses-
sion, devised simply and solely with
a view to enable Sir LANGHAM DALE
to remain at his post until a suc-
cessor was appointed; and we pre-
sume then that, with the appoint
ment of Dr. MUIR, the makeshift
goes. This, at all events, by the
way. As if to complete the chan-
ges, Mr. GEO. MACONACHIE, for
many years Secretary to the Educa-
tion Office, has been removed by
death’s grim hand, and is mourned
by a large body of men connected
with the Education Department
who had become familiar with his
handwriting on all the documents
from headquarters. To those whose
duties led them into this office when
in Capetown the associations will
be novel indeed with new occupants
tenanting the well-known compart-
ments. Of course the changes can-
not but be without effect on the
Educational policy of the country,
and people have to wait to see
whether the impression they are
likely to leave will be good or bad
It is sincerely to be hoped that
these changes may go far to secure
only the increased usefulness of this
important branch of the institutions
of the country; and we can wish
the new officials nothing better than
the good fortune to have applied to
them, after a fair trial of their
work, the virtue usually ascribed to
new brooms. Sir LANGHAM DALE
does not actually sever connection
with the office until his successor
arrives ; there will, therefore, be
time enough for the country to pay
him the well-merited tribute for
long self-denying labours in the
cause of enlightenment in this
land.

CONGRESSIONAL RULE

  OR some time past we have
  been at pains to show that

Mr. HOFMEYR has so carefully con-
solidated his party in the House of
Assembly that ho now wields a
solid automatic force of thirty-four
(Bondmen more or less) who are
quite ready at his beck and call to
vote whatever Franchise proposal
he might elect to carry through
the House. The remaining forty-
one members consist of two,
three, or more small factions, fol-
lowing as many leaders. One
faction swears by Sir GORDON
SPRIGG, and would follow him to
whichever side of the House he
went; another has placed Mr.
SAUER at its head ; but as it con-
sists of the able, intelligent, and con-
sistent element in the House, it
cannot be said that it would follow
its Leader wherever he goes; and
hitherto the leader himself has not
done anything to place himself in a
position in which his followers
could not back him; lastly the
Premier claims a few personal
followers. As has been shown and
no attempt made to controvert it,
a defection of four from the
congeries of parties composing the
fourty-one, joining Mr. HOF-
MEYR’S well-drilled force of thirty-
four, would ensure the necessary
majority to make the Member for
Stellenbosch the head of the most
powerful and independent Ministry
that has ever held office since the
days of Mr. MOLTENO.

But what would be the meaning
of such elevation of the Bond
party ? Why; Parliament, with an
Executive dominated by Bondmen,
would be reduced to the position of
a body existing only for the
purpose of registering the de-
crees of the annual Bond Con-
gress. What that implies we
will not attempt to enlarge on to-
day ; but this is one serious objec-
tion of those who prize British
freedom to any change in the
Franchise that will suit the real
aims of the Bond. Should the
Ministry gratify the Bond, a state
of things will be established, under
which independent members might

as well remain at their homes and wire their votes; since argument will
go for nothing with a majority bound to vote to the order of the
Congress, on pain of dissolution. We wonder how those will like it who
prate about a change which is not called for by the exigencies of the
time, not knowing that they are playing into the hands of an
associ­ation sworn to work in the dark.

suitable addresses were made by Letsea’s
successor Lerotholi, Sir Marshall Clarke
and others.

YET another sign of the
NATIVE PROGRESS progress that our people
are quietly though surely
making in the economy of this country.
This time it comes from Cradock. It
seems to be the practice in this district
for the Divisional Council to sell the lease
of tolls and pounds ; and it is gratifying
to find that no less than three Natives
entered as bidders with the result that
the highest price for any one toll was bid
by a Native, Piet Guzuna for the Kafir’s
Leegte ; the toll being knocked down to
him for £260; the next, Moordenaar’s
Nek, fell to John B. Makanana for £199
also a couneryman of ours; and another
Marais Sluit was secured by Boy KWAZA
for £50. We believe that this is not the
first year that they have become the
leasers.
