  THE Natives of the East London Division are entitled to the best
  thanks of our countrymen for their wise decision to present Captain
  Brabant, M.L.A., C.M.G., with an address, thanking him
  for the enduring services he rendered to the cause of justice and of
  the natives during the late Parliamentary Session. Natives are always
  charged by superficial observers with want of gratitude; and it is
  well that they should occasionally give the lie to this calumny, when
  sold and disinterested services have been given in their behalf.
  Overwhelming as have been the forces that invaded native rights last
  session, it is gratifying to remember that our people have staunch,
  friends among the most eminent poli- ticians in the Colony, and the
  sympathy of the great majority of the English people. As one of these
  politicians, then, Captain Brabant is to be than/ced by the East
  London Natives. We hope to give an account of the proceedings that are
  expected to ta&e place next Saturday at the gallant gentleman’s
  residence.

  As additional evidence to show that the inclusion of land in the
  making up of the £25 franchise was by design and not by accident, may
  be cited the following which occurs in a Despatch of the Right Hon.
  Sir John Pakington, the DuA-e df Newcastle’s immediate predecessor in
  the Colonial Office, dated September 4, 1852. Writing to Governor
  Lieutenant General the Hon. G. Cathcart, Sir John Pakington observed:
  “ It is extremely doubtlul whether the restriction of the
  Franchise to the occupation of buildings of the value of £25 only,
  without taking into account land occupied therewith, may not create a
  very narrow and restricted right of voting.” Hence the occupation of
  land was included in the eighth section of the Constitution Ordinance,
  but the native is carefully deprived of it in the Act of the late
  session. To this the attention of Her Majesty’s Government will be
  called in the Petition of the natives to Her Majesty.

  WE are gratified that the movement against the disfranchisement
  question is progressing satisfactorily among the natives. In addition
  to Pore Elizabeth and King Williamstown the Queenstown and Glen Grey
  natives have joined in it. So also have Herschel Fort Beaufort and
  Emigrant Tembuland. Peddie is making preparations to have a meeting.
  The natives feel that even if they may not succeed in getting the
  Secretary of State to refuse the Royal Assent to the
  Disfranchising Act, still the duty of protest devolves upon them, and
  it is for them to discharge it.
