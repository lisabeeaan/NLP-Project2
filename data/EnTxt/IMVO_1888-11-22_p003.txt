to be represented maintain a large
Native population, it is absolutely
important that the candidate for the
seat should be a man who holds no
narrow and restricted views to-
wards this large section of the
Frontier population.

IT is gratifying to observe that the
public conscience in Great Britain is
being aroused to the horrors of the Slave
Trade that the Arabs are plying in
Central Africa.—Commander Cameron,
R.N., recently delivered a lecture at
East Bourne, on the subject of “ Slavery
in South Africa ” in the course of which
he stated that 2,000,000 Africans are
killed every year through the slave
trade; and added that one of two things
must happen—either the traffic in slaves
must be put down with a strong hand,
or Africa will be depopulated. This
statement is only too true as recent
accounts of the horrible brutality of the
Arab slave hunters and their cannibal
envoys show. The meeting above refer-
red to, resolved to form a society, to be
called the Gordon-Livingstone Society,
to collect funds to send anti-slavery
expedition or expeditions to Central
Africa. The European powers, it is
reported, are organising a thorough
blockade of the slave coasts, which if
rigorously pursued will doubtless
effectually cripple the nefarious traffic.

REV. CANON TAYLOR, a London divine,
a short while ago caused a fluttering in
Missionary dovecots by expressing it as
his deliberate opinion that Mahommed-
anism was being more rapidly propagated
among the benighted population ot the
African continent than Christianity.
The Canon put this down to the general
inactivity, arising from the want of fire
in Christian Missionaries, to win Africans
for their Master as soon as they can,
while the apostles of the Mahamedan
faith were straining every nerve to
secure Africa. Canon Taylor, in the
face of these facts made the bold
suggestion that the Societies in Great
Britain should withdraw their mission-
aries and abandon Africa to Islam. This
suggestion has drawn down a hornet’s
nest on the Rev. Canon, who, nothing
daunted, goes on exposing missionary
Failures in one of the Reviews. He

  reckons that about £2,000,000 of good
  British gold is spent in keeping up
  missionary establishments. A liberal
  allowance makes the total number of
  converts not more than 60,000; while the
  annual increase of the unconverted
  heathen population is eleven millions

of souls. At this rate, the heathen are not likely to be converted until
the sun's heat has cooled and the earth is a used up planet. The Canon
is, therefore, Very strongly of opinion that the £2,000,000 a year is
wasted. Allowing for individual exceptions, the missionary service, he
contends, is practically a system of outdoor relief for clerical
failures, or, in the pithy Scotch phrase, “ stick it ministers.” The
Canon adds:—“IfSt. Paul, before start­ing on one of his missionary
journeys, had required St. James and a committee at Jerusalem to
guarantee him £300 a year, paid quarterly, and had provided himself with
a shady bungalow, a punkah, a pony carriage, and a wife, he could not
have changed the history of the world.”

Now, it is for those who are interested • in the success of Christian
Missions to profit by such criticisms; and if there has been anything to
warrant them to see that it is removed. Canon Taylor is no unfriendly
critic by any means; and we trust the result of his criticism will be to
make missionaries to redouble their efforts and put in more energy in
advanc­ing the cause which they have at heart.

THE elections which came off last week are those of Kimberley,
Worcester, Albany, Queenstown, Fort Beaufort, Colesberg, Caledon, the
Cape Division, and Barkly West, For Kimberley, Messrs. Barnato, Lange,
Lynch, and O’Leary were returned ; Worcester, re­turned Messrs. J. I. de
Villiers and de Vos; Albany, Messrs. Norton and Trower; Queenstown,
Messrs. Frost and du Plessis; Colesberg, Messrs, van der Walt and
Venter; Fort Beaufort, Messrs. Laing and Hutton; Caledon, Mr. Beyers and
Sir T. Upington ; The Cape Division, Dr. Smuts and Mr. Innes ; Victoria
East, Dr. Pope and Mr. Johnson ; and in Barkly West, it is not doubted
that Mr. Rhodes and Mr. Paton are elected.

WITH one exception—Mr. O’Leary—the Kimberley representation has
undergone a radical change. Gentlemen who are claimed by the three
Parliamentary parties have been returned, but inas­much as Mr. Rhodes
will have much to say in shaping their political course, and as Mr.
Rhodes is of the Opposition we may fairly put them down as three
Oppositionists. In that case the Ministry loses. Two Bond men are to sit
for Worcester. One Ministerialist and one Bondman for Albany; Queenstown
ditto. Two Oppositionists for Fort Beaufort, as Mr. Laing will not, like
Captain Brabant, surrender his conscience to Sir Gordon Sprigg. Two
Bondmen, as before, will represent Colesberg. At Caledon a
Ministerialist and a Bondman have been elected, where formerly two
Ministerial­ists sat. The Cape Division remains Opposition, so does West
Barkly. On the whole, after taking stock of the losses and gains to
parties, the elections of the week cannot be said to have affected the
balance of parties in the House.

MINISTERIALISTS are naturally most anxious to increase their forces.
There can be no objection to this desire. We observe, however, that some
of the sup­port they claim is mere padding. For instance, they number
among themselves Captain Brabant, who on being asked to pledge himself
to say “ he was a follower of Sir G. Sprigg] and trust him to give the
key note,” said, in the face of the Premier “ his objection was that he
pre­ferred to be the keeper of his own con­science . . . and he would
have been in an utterly false position in regard to the Registration
Act, for instance, had he been so bound.” Messrs. Lange, Wiener, Lynch,
Barnato, are more or less indis- criminately put down as supporters ot
the Sprigg Government, but on grounds not stronger than those on which
Captain iBrabant is claimed.
