IT is stated that a Mr. Ayliff, from Natal, has been appointed to be
Superin­tendent of Fin goes at Heald Town in the room of Mr. Dugmore,
whose services have been dispensed with after an inquiry. The salary
attached to the post is £225 per annum. The Heald Town Natives know that

shibboleth, having fallen vacant it would have been allowed to drop.

THE Bond Organ (Burghersdorp) sup­ports the exclusion of Natives from
the Franchise on the ground that the Natives “ regard all South Africa
as their birth­right, and if the opportunity ever offered, they would
subordinate the whites to their rule.” In the opinion of the Bond Organ
this will be the result of the alliance of the English and the Natives.
Now, this is a mistake. If the Venters, the Le Roexs, and De Wets of our
Parliament were supplanted by Natives to-morrow the country would at
once have at least a decorous body sitting in Cape Town, only solicitous
of finding the truth and meting out justice to English, Dutch, and Kafir
alike. This much cannot be said of our. Dutch neighbours, who care
nothing for the English or the Native so long as their interests are
served.

IN answer to Mr. Innes’ question refer­ring to Mabandla, Mr. De Wet has
said “Government did not intend to reinstate Mabandla. After the receipt
of Mr. Dick’s report further information was received of a serious
nature as to Mabandla’s con­duct, and the best service his friends could
do him was to leave the matter alone, for he might congratulate himself
that he had not been criminally pro­secuted.” We are sincerely sorry
that this question was put late in the session, as otherwise papers on
the subject might have been called for which would, we have not the
slightest doubt, show that Mr. De Wet’s statement about subsequent and
serious information respecting Maba­ndla’s conduct was purely
apocryphal. For our part we long ago gave up the nope that Mabandla
would receive any justice at the hands of the present Secretary for
Native Affairs after the zigzag and tortuous course he pursued on this
simple matter; and we looked to Parliament to inquire into the matter,
and we do so still. As for Mr. De Wet, all the Natives will be quite
able to endorse the remarks of the P. E. Telegraph when itsaid when
dealing with the Brandy question: “ Mr. De Wet is a Dutchman, and a
Dutchman suddenly- taking such a parental interest, and ex­hibiting such
zeal for the rights and pri­vileges of the Kafirs would be about the
most exquisite joke, not merely of the session, but of the century.”
