  ZiYHAT the political views of the general run of people in the
  Colony are circumscribed has

recently been made clear by articles which have appeared in some
newspapers, supposed to be in­fluential, about Mr. J. C. MOLTENO’S
candidature for Tembuland. These prints have virulently assailed Mr.
SAUER, in particular and Messrs. MERRIMAN and INNES in general for
having put forward Mr. MOL­TENO for Tembuland; the action being regarded
as unheard of “dictation.” One journal—the Queenstown Free Press—has
even gone so far in its exasperation as to belabour NATIVE OPINION with
its Editor for daring to advocate the return of that gentleman. Now, we
confess we don’t think much of the attitude of the Free Press in this
particular matter; and we refer to our contemporary because it was
beginning to earn the respect and affection of the more intelligent
section of the Frontier communities for the noble and fearless stand it
had fur some time taken up for the lifting up of politics to a purer and
more elevated atmosphere. At Queenstown the Free Press has been the most
strenuous and devoted supporter of Dr. BERRY’S, the most conspicuous
light in the Eastern political firmament, and a gentle­man than whom
none has been more forward in expounding sound doc­trine in respect of
the observance of the true principles of Respons­ible Government as
carried out in the mother country, from which our representative
institutions are copied. And what do we find there ? Certainly none of
the pettifogging spirit that would prevent Mr. MOLTENO from
repre­senting Tembuland. In England it is no uncommon thing for the
furtherest provincial seat in the House of Commons to be occupied by a
Londoner ; and indeed theie the question of the residence of a candidate
pales into utter insigni­ficance by the side of the colour of the
politics of the candidate. But ordinary Cape Colonial intelligence would
accpt the reverse of this. A man’s views and opinions go for nothing
when the question of his residence is put into the scale. We are
surprised that it should be the Port Elizabeth Telegraph, a journal
which lays itself out to in­struct its readers in English politics,
which should raise an outcry against Mr. MOLTENO for being a Western man
; while we are not surprised at the East London Dis­patch nibbling at
the question in its own peculiar style. For our­selves the residence of
a candidate is a very small matter so long as his views are in the main
sound and satisfactory. It has been no wrench for us to heartily support
Mr. MOLTENO in Tembuland against men residing in the Eastern Dis­tricts
whose views—if they had any views at all—appeared to us crude and
unsatisfactory. Then again fault has been found with Messrs. SAUER,
INNES and MERRIMAN for having recommended Mr. MOLTENO, a candidate
representing their views to the electors of Tembuland. Mr. SAUER,
especially, has been roundly abused for having entered Tembuland to
support that gentle­man’s candidature. This also appears to us very
small and utterly unworthy of the P. E. Telegraph's intell gence.
Somebody must move in any case in connection with the putting up of a
man for Par­liament; and no men have a better right to secure candidates
for the various constituencies than the leaders of Parties in the House.
They have to bear the brunt of the Parliamentary battles and they are
entitled, wherever they can, to put up candidates who, when returned,
would render them assistance. Now, Tembuland, covering a large Native
Territory, is a division which requires its representative to be in
close touch with gentlemen like Mr. SAUER and Mr. INNES who take a great
interest in the welfare of the Natives. Col. GRIFFITH has held that
relation to them until he was dazzled by Mr. RHODES’S brilliancy to such
an extent as to attach himself to his person­ality forgetting his duty
to that party which has always stood by the Natives. Should the
gentle­men of this party have sat idle while they saw a seat which they
could secure carried by a gentle­man identifying himself with an
opposing political camp I Non­sense ! Time and again have we seen in
England the political leaders put forward their nominees and going to
the trouble of advo­cating their return for various seats; but in the
Colony there would appear to be people who regard so necessary a
political duty as an enormity ! Away with such politicians ! who do not
understand the value of political combination, and would therefore leave
Mr. HOFMEYR alone to profit by that useful instrument while they return
flabby members who are neither fish, fowl, nor good red herring.
