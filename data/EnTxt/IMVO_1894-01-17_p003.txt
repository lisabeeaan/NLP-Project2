NATIVE OPINION

THE ELECTION IN QUEENSTOWN

  N a spirited article the Queenstown Representative rebukes us because
  of our attitude toward Messrs and ROGERS and FROST in connection with
  the Parliamentary election in its district. Let us premise the remarks
  we wish to make on this subject by stating that our contemporary is a
  warm supporter of Mr. ROGERS’S candidature, whom it should like to see
  returned along with Dr. BERRY.
  Thus it will be seen that whilst both our contemporary and ourselves
  are agreed on Dr. BERRY. we are at issue on Mr. ROGERS and Mr. FROST
  each of whom is a hanger-on to the Bond. It must be borne in mind that
  those of the electors of Queenstown who thought it necessary to use
  their second vote had before them in Messrs. FROST and ROGERS a choice
  of two evils. The Representative advises them to take Mr. ROGERS
  because, as would now appear, he is right on the Glen Grey question.
  For ourselves we recommended Mr.FROST for the purposes of maintaining
  the alliance, which we trust may last, with the Progressive farmers.
  It is no mean thing for those gentlemen to have departed
  from their old traditions of opposing all candidates who held fair and
  liberal views towards the Natives in order to support Dr. BERRY, and
  we thought it would be a good
  thing for our people to recognize this by helping them to return their
  favourite Mr. FROST. We leave it to others to judge as to the force of
  the respective arguments.
  We wish, however, to say once for all, that we are totally indifferent
  to the return of either Mr. R GERS or Mr FROST, notwithstanding the
  argument if the Representative conveyed in the following :—

  Where is IMVO'S freedom of thoughtand action when he advocates the
  support of Mr. Frost “although tacitly serving
  the ends of the Bond” for the sake of alliance with farmers? IMVO
  admits Mr, Frost's views to be heterodox on the Glen Grey- question ;
  Mr. Roger's views on that question he knows to be orthodox and yet
  with this infinitely important Native*question looming out ominously
  in the future session, hanging like the sword of Damocles over the
  heads of the
  whole Kafir race—IMVO erects the standard of heterodoxy and calls upon
  his people to rally round it, while he tramples the banner of
  orthodoxy under his feet. And why? Because he considers it better to
  vote for the wrong man than risk losing a few votes. He reminds us of
  the character in Bleak
  House who suggests it is better to hang “the w-rong fellar, than no
  fellar at all.” It is impossible for the Natives to support
  Mr. Frost. By so doing they would sanction the egregious blunder he
  committed in the Glen Grey business and the ruin it then threatened
  ruin, which though happily averted far the time being, is still a
  danger to them in the near future. We know IMVO. too well to think he
  will plead the cause of heterodoxy against orthodoxy.

  Let us at once concede the plausibility of our contemporary’s
  argument. Still we fail to feel its force. If it were once admitted
  that one
  may pick and choose plums in a candidate’s manifesto, in the manner
  the Representative has done in respect of Mr. ROGERS’S views on
  Glen Grey, we foresee great difficulties in arriving at a satisfactory
  conclusion in the discussion of public questions. Hence our well-
  known disposition to shun the examination of the views of those who
  stand before the electors for Parliamentary honours ; and merely exact
  an answer as to the Party a
  candidate intends to co operate with in the House ; for It is of the
  very essence of Responsible Government that individual members must
  work

by and through parties if they are to succeed in carrying anything
through a House composed of seventy-six members. Judged by the standard
of Party, then, Mr. ROGERS fails to give satisfaction. He may hold
orthodox views on many subjects likely to come before Parliament, but we
maintain he cannot make headway in advancing them by isolating himself
as one independent of Party, or by pre­tending under that title to
possess so comprehensive a knowledge of things as to dispense with
consulting others. The thing is absurd. A member without confidential
friends with whom to consult and co-operate is a miserable creature
deserving of public contempt So, also, is he who professes saving
convictions, but hopes to give effect them by and through a party
entirely out of sympathy with those views. Hence our quarrel with the
Inde­pendent candidates so called ; and with those who enunciating
satisfac­tory views end by joining with parties notoriously opposed to
their views.

  S is well known Mr. J. C-MOLTENO is at the present

moment making a tour through the electoral Division of Tembuland in the
interests of the New Party of Principle in Parliament. He has just taken
the field to glean support where three gentlemen owing allegiance to
other and opposing Parties have for months past been working. It will be
a matter of great satisfaction to all the friends of the Party to fight
the battle of which Mr. MOLTENO has gone into Tembuland, to learn that
by his able, straight, and well- directed speeches he is carrying
everything before him. Mr. MOLTENO opened his campaign at St. Mark’s on
Saturday week by addressing a—for that village—very large European
meeting. Although other candidates had been there, yet they have not
been accorded a meeting anything so
large. The result was that many who had not pledged themselves gladly
promised him support, whilst some who had early committed themselves
were sorry they had done so. On the Monday following, Southeyville was
visited. This ward had not been looked up by any of Mr. MOLTENO’S
rivals; the consequence was that he gathered in all the European votes
in that vicinity. At both these places leading men among Natives paid
the candidate their respects, testifying to the good wishes of the
neighbouring Natives for his success. But the most brilliant
success scored by Mr. MOLTENO was on the 10th instant, when the largest
Native meeting took place at Cala to see and hear the candidate who was
here joined by the
Hon. J. AV. SAUER. For his long unflinching devotion to the cause of
right and justice towards the Natives, in times of trouble and of peace.
Mr. SAUER is undoubtedly the most popular politician in this country
among that great section
of the community. He is loved and respected by them far and near, his
name being a household word among these people The opponents of Mr.
MOLTENO had done not a little by promises and honied speeches to wheedle
some of the
Native voters; but the appearance of Mr. SAUER on the scene cleared,
the way for Mr. MOLTENO’S adoption,
by the Natives. Th s great gathering, after listening to the weighty and
well-put words of Messrs. SAUER and MOLTENO, heartily and most
enthusiastically resolved that all Natives throughout the electoral
division should vote for Mr.MOLTENO, and steps be taken to secure his
return. These gentlemen afterwards met the European electors in the
Court room, and promises of unexpected support poured in upon the lucky
candidate. Those who know the constituency of Tembuland know that more
than half the voters are in the area Mr. MOLTENO has traversed, whilst
the rest 600 are scattered over the wide area consisting of Fingoland
and Tembuland
Proper. Taking the number of the Natives who, to their credit, have
resolved in a body to support their friends in Parliament by supporting
the candidate those friends have accredited, Mr. MOLTENO is certain of
support from more than half the voters already, and judging by the
phenominal success with which he is meeting, wherever he goes, he should
come in very easily indeed. Thia should serve as a salutary lesson to
those who desire to represent constituencies with large numbers of
voters to consider well their views before coming forward ; and of this
they may be assured that double-faced men, trying to ride two horses at
the same time, need not apply.
