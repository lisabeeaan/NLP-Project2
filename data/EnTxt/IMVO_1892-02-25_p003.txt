FUDGE

  FTER a week’s excogitation
  of a reply to an article

which appeared in our impression a
fortnight ago on the proceedings be-
fore Mr. HOLLAND in connection
with the Registration, the Kaffra-
rian Watchman has attempted a
leader made up of arguments the
most milk-and-watery that have
ever been offered as mental pabu-
lum to political babes. We should
not be noticing the composition now
but for the excuse it affords to em-
phasis once more the glaring de-
fects of the lamentable decision, in
an effort to defend which the journal
which bears the device “ Nunquam
Dormio ” seems determined to ex-
haust all its grotesque exercises at
evasion. As is now pretty generally
known that the findings of the
Civil Commissioner were based on
Mr. BROSTER’S opinion, the Watch-
man very properly strains every
nerve to establish that gentleman’s
bona fides. But, very unfortunately
for it, it has failed most egregiously
to do so. In the place of honest,
straightforward argument, we are
treated to ill-concealed tricks. Let
us for a moment address ourselves
to the extremely easy task of tear-
ing off the veil under which it is
sought to hide the honest facts of
the case. It is deemed importan
to tell us that “ an examination of
“ the public records of the Registra-
“ tration Court shows no such
“ testimony given by Mr. BROSTER
“ as to setting a value of £16 upon
“ six-acre lots.” May we ask our
contemporary, at whose disposal
“ the public records ” seem to have
been placed, why, when, and by whom
was this examination of the records
made ? Whether it was before or
after Mr. HOLLAND’S decision was
given? And, if before, whether it
was because there existed a doubt as
to Mr. BROSTER’S previous valua-
tion of the Natives’ lots as given
before Mr CHALMERS. If, how-
ever, the examination of the re-
cords has taken place since the
ruling of the Civil Commissioner,
then, pray, has it been from any
wish to in a measure redress the
grievance of our people if it was
discovered that Mr. BROSTER did
say that the value of the Natives’
erven was above £6 ? That before
Mr. CHALMERS Mr. BROSTER ap-
praised these very lots at £2 10s
per acre needed no reference to the
public records at the Civil Commis-
sioner’s Court at all; as the whole
information could—without under-
taking a pilgrimage to the Court
involving the self-imposed and
thankless task of rummaging
through dusty, musty, archives there
—have been found within the boards
of the Watchman’s file of 1890, in
the issue of our contemporary of
January 20 of that year of Grace.
Therein it is recorded as follows;
“At the Court on Tuesday
“ the Sworn Appraiser valued the
“ land at £2 10s. per acre, and the
“ Magistrate accepted this.” Does
the Watchman pretend to deny that
this was ever uttered in the Court
of Justice of Kingwilliamstown by
Mr. BROSTER, the Sworn Apprai-
ser ? But such affectation of igno-
rance does not appear to have
availed the Watchman.

  “ Oh, what a tangled web we weave
  When first we study to deceive !”

In the sentence following that
denying all existence of the record,
it is artlessly stated by the writer
that “ Mr. BROSTER was not sworn
“ by Mr. W. B. CHALMERS but
'' simply made answer to a casual
“ question put to him, and, we are
“ assured, this is not on the record
“ of the proceedings of the former
“ Registration Court.” Of course
nobody expected that it would
be there, after what the Watchman
has itself previously said, that the
record was not discovered after the
diligent search that had been in-
stituted. After trying on this
argument our contemporary itself
feels oppressed with a sense of its
incoherence, and proceeds to adopt
another, hardly more ingenious than
ingenuous, but to our mind even
weaker than the first. Our con-
temporary's next effort at argument
leaves it on the horns of an awk-
ward dilemma, and it is compelled
to affirm what it did not intend—
that the Natives’ lands are absolutely ;
valueless. We read :—

A field of six acres would in a
very good season, reckoning three muids
to an acre, yield eighteen bags of mealies,
and in good times, when mealies are
plentiful, they seldom obtain for them
more than 5s. a bag. Th is represents a
return of £4 10s, for the year, but from
that sum must be deducted the expense
of ploughing and sowing well as the
cost of the seed, say altogether £1. To
this must be added 18s.. for scoffling six
acres twice at 1s. 6d. the acre, and 10s., the
expense of harvesting and carrying to
market eighteen bags. We have thus
£2 8s. to take away from the annual re-
turn of £4 10s., and there is then left
£2 2s.; and if what is termed a fair rental
or six acres, namely £2, has further to be

deducted, two shillings actually only re­mains as the yearly return from
a six-acre lot.

Well, we should not like to insult the intelligence of our readers by
demonstrating the utter absurdity of this. But we would repeat the
question we put in former references to this subject, why the actual
cur­rent rents, ranging from £2 10s to £5 annually, and the market
values from £25 to £35 of these lots, are passed by; and resort had to
ridiculous and impossible calcu­lations which, at best, are
hy­pothetical and of no value at all. Further down in the Watchman
article we are treated to an argu­ment on the Valuation Roll of the
Divisional Council, which shows that the value of these lots have,
within the past three or four years been jerrymandered, without rhyme or
reason, by militant and aggressive political partisans from £22 10s. to
£7. Really it is no wonder one feels tantalised to be invited to combat
frivolous and inconsistent talk of this character, and we must be
ex­cused if we apply to it the classic expression of Squire BURCHELL, “
Fudge ! ”

GREY HOSPITAL.

  T is with no small degree of
  alarm that the Natives will

learn that changes are taking place
in connection with the Grey Hospi-
tal, Kingwilliamstown, the aim and
purport of which may be the
??? dgment of the advantages and
privileges which our people have
enjoyed from the foundation of the
Institution. The Hospital was
erected by Sir GEORGE GREY prin-
cipally with a view to benefit the
Natives in an especial degree. For
the English and Dutch sections of
the community he set up Colleges
for the education of the rising
generation ; while to the blacks,
among other gifts, he devised this
Hospital. It is no matter for sur-
prise then that our people should
look upon it as peculiarly their own ;
and we do hope and trust that Gov-
ernment will do nothing to impair
the capabilities of the institution to
do further service to our people.
But we confess to an uneasy feeling
as to the future of Grey Hospital,
due to a fear that the powers that
be do not fully recognise the claims
the Natives have on the place, and
the deep interest they take in it.
Ever since the retirement of good
Dr. FITZGERALD rumours of change
in the management of the Hospital
have filled the air. But we have
consoled ourselves with the reflec-
tion that whatever change was
affected under a Colonial Secretary
not unfriendly to our people would
not be such as to render the insti-
tution less beneficial than it has
been to the Natives. The matter
seemed settled by Mr. SAUER’S
answer last Session to Mr. SCHERM-
BRUCKER, the Junior Member for
Kingwilliamstown. ''The Colonel''
had asked whether the changes
spoken of as being likely to be
effected in the conduct of Grey
Hospital were mooted by the Gov-
ernment. The Colonial Secretary
had arrived at no conclusion on the
subject; and we were led to infer
matters would go on as before.
Then followed Dr. BLAINE’S ap-
pointment as successor to Dr. FITZ-
GERALD—an appointment which, by
the way, does not appear to have
been popular with the Natives. To
this step is to be added another
which bears a suspicious look about
it. We learn that all the old em-
ployes of Grey Hospital have re-
ceived notice that their services will
be dispensed with in another month
or two. All we can say at this
stage of the matter—a matter which
is likely to cause a stir among the
Natives in the neighbourhood as
great as any that has happened be-
fore—is to express sincere regret
that Government seems to have
resolved upon dealing with the
Hospital in an off-hand fashion as if
no broad claims of policy were in-
volved in their action; and we do
hope Mr. SAUER may be dissuaded
from acting in a hasty manner in
what he intends to do with the Hos-
pital, as vested interests have to be
considered in any new scheme of
management.

A CABINET DECISION.

THE evidently inspired report to which currency has been given by
Capetown newspapers that the Ministry has arrived at a decison on the
vexed question of the Franchise has been received with satisfaction in
more quarters than one. The details of the Government measure to make
due provision for the due representation of the material and educational
in- terests in the Constitution will be made known a fortnight hence by
Mr. SAUER at Aliwal North. There is thus no occa­sion to anticipate the
disclosures of the Honourable Gentleman. It

is something that the Great Amal­gamator—the present Premier— should
have so managed matters as to arrive at a decision without sacrificing
any member of his Ministry; and it must be the most moderate measure it
was possible to devise which could secure the ap­proval of such
liberal-minded poli­ticians as Mr. SAUER, Mr. ROSE- INNEB and Mr.
MERRIMAN. We shall all be curious to hear what it is that they have been
induced to take as the irreducible minimum of change in the Franchise.
That the settlement of the question offered by the Ministry is to rest
entirely on its own merits, quite independ­ent of Mr. HOFMEYR’S likes
and dislikes, is evidenced by this gentleman’s disclaimer to the effect
that he has not bound himself to support a part of the Government scheme
; and asking the newspapers to state that he is not at present prepared
to support a mea­sure having for its object the introduction of the
Ballot. It seems from this that the Gov­ernment will assume an attitude
on the subject not different from that taken up in respect of the Liquor
legislation of last year, when they insisted upon Local Option, and
succeed in the teeth of the powerful opposition of the Member for
Stellenbosch. But we shall see.
